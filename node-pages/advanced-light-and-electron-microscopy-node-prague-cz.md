# Advanced Light and Electron Microscopy Node Prague

## Overview

The Advanced Light and Electron Microscopy Node Prague mainly offers advanced light microscopy, including super-resolution, live-cell and functional fluorescence methods. It also offers biological electron microscopy and correlative workflows that place a fluorescent signal in its ultrastructural context. The Node brings together seven facilities at six institutions in Prague, Vestec and České Budějovice, and it covers biological imaging as well as preclinical work in small animals.

Staff at the EM facility in Prague prepare cell cultures, tissues, worms and microorganisms for electron microscopy, using chemical fixation, high-pressure and plunge freezing, and cryo-FIB milling. The LEM team in České Budějovice develops methods with collaborators, such as conductive resins for serial block-face SEM. At the Imaging Methods Core Facility in Vestec, staff advise on experimental design and analysis for light and electron microscopy of samples from macromolecules to organoids. The IPHYS Bio-Imaging Facility in Prague brings biologists, engineers and mathematicians together to customise microscopes and measure the mechanical properties of samples. IFIEB in Prague images plants under near-natural conditions, and the LM facility in Prague specialises in super-resolution and quantitative phase imaging. Staff at the Microscopy Service Centre in Prague carry out rodent surgery and cranial-window preparation for intravital imaging.

Confocal and spinning-disk microscopy are offered at every Prague and Vestec facility except the EM facility. Stimulated emission depletion (STED) microscopy is at IFIEB, the Imaging Methods Core Facility and the LM facility. Electron microscopy is at the EM facility, LEM and the Imaging Methods Core Facility, and correlative light and electron microscopy is at the EM facility and the Imaging Methods Core Facility. Intravital microscopy is at the IPHYS Bio-Imaging Facility, the Imaging Methods Core Facility and the Microscopy Service Centre, while micro-PET/CT is at the IPHYS Bio-Imaging Facility. Long-term imaging of plants on a vertical stage is at IFIEB.

## Imaging technologies offered

Fluorescence microscopy lets researchers follow cells and tissues from whole organs down to single molecules. Confocal, spinning-disk and image scanning microscopy give multicolour optical sections of cells and tissues. Two-photon microscopy at the IPHYS Bio-Imaging Facility, the Imaging Methods Core Facility and the Microscopy Service Centre reaches cells deep in thick tissue and in living animals. For nanoscale detail, STED confines fluorescence to structures below the diffraction limit. Single-molecule localisation microscopy (SMLM) maps the positions of individual molecules at the LM facility, the Microscopy Service Centre and the Imaging Methods Core Facility. FLIM, FRET and fluorescence correlation spectroscopy measure molecular interactions, environment and diffusion, and they are offered at IFIEB, the LM facility and the Imaging Methods Core Facility.

Light-sheet microscopy images embryos and whole organs at the IPHYS Bio-Imaging Facility, the LM facility and the Microscopy Service Centre. Electron microscopy covers TEM of chemically fixed and cryo-immobilised samples, immunogold labelling to locate proteins, and cryo-EM methods. For volume imaging, LEM offers serial block-face SEM and array tomography, and the Imaging Methods Core Facility offers FIB-SEM.

The Microscopy Service Centre runs a Femtonics FEMTO3D Atlas for intravital imaging. IFIEB has a vertical-stage Zeiss LSM880 with Airyscan, so roots keep growing upright while they are imaged. At LEM, a JEOL JEM-2100F TEM and a Verios 5 UC SEM with a cryo system and elemental analysis cover transmission and scanning work.

## Specialties and research focus

IFIEB in Prague serves plant biology. Its imaging follows root growth and cellular development in plants kept under near-natural conditions, at spatial and temporal scales from single cells to growing organs.

Cell biology and structural biology are the main fields at the EM facility and LEM. The EM facility works with human and animal cell cultures, plant and animal tissues, worms and microorganisms, and also with lipid micelles. The Imaging Methods Core Facility handles macromolecules, microbes, cells, tissues and organoids for both light and electron microscopy, and it brings fluorescence and ultrastructure together in correlative projects.

Preclinical and small-animal research is supported at the IPHYS Bio-Imaging Facility, which also studies the mechanical properties of biological samples. The Microscopy Service Centre works with small rodents in a BSL2 laboratory, where intravital studies follow cellular processes in their intact physiological context. At the LM facility, the focus is on advanced light microscopy, super-resolution and quantitative phase imaging of cells and subcellular structures. The Node's seven facilities work closely together, and the Node runs training courses in both basic and advanced microscopy.

## Services and user support

A project at the Node starts with methodological setup. Staff help design the study protocol and write standard operating procedures, so the imaging plan fits the biological question before any data are collected.

Researchers can then be trained to use the instruments themselves, with training workstations and a training seminar room supporting this teaching. Alternatively, staff provide technical assistance and run the instruments together with the visiting researcher. For work on samples, the Node provides wet lab space and cell culture facilities at safety levels 1 and 2. In vivo projects are supported by animal facilities and housing facilities. The Node also offers biobanking, with storage and processing of biological material, which helps when a project runs over several visits or needs samples kept between sessions.

Once images are acquired, staff help with data processing and analysis, from processing raw data to quantification. The Node provides server space for this work, and data storage keeps the results secure during the project.

Three facilities offer electron microscopy. The EM facility concentrates on sample preparation and ultrastructural imaging, including cryo-EM methods, cryo-FIB milling and immunogold labelling on thawed cryo-sections. LEM covers the whole route from choosing a preparation method to image processing, with volume imaging by serial block-face SEM and array tomography, cryo-SEM and immunogold labelling on resin sections. The Imaging Methods Core Facility combines light and electron microscopy, with FIB-SEM, cryo-fluorescence and correlative workflows, and it adds image analysis to its methods.

Light microscopy is spread across the Prague facilities and the Imaging Methods Core Facility. The LM facility offers fluorescence methods from deconvolution and high-content screening to STED and tissue clearing, and its staff work with many analysis software packages. The Microscopy Service Centre combines SMLM and TIRF with intravital two-photon imaging of small rodents in its BSL2 laboratory. The IPHYS Bio-Imaging Facility offers light-sheet imaging and optical projection tomography, label-free harmonic generation and mechanical measurements, together with micro-PET/CT and in vivo optical imaging for preclinical work.

IFIEB is the Node's plant imaging facility. It offers plant-optimised confocal and STED microscopy, lifetime and FRET measurements, and long-term imaging on a vertical stage that follows root growth without turning the plant on its side.
