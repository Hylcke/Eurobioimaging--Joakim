# Advanced Light and Electron Microscopy Node Prague

## Overview

The Advanced Light and Electron Microscopy Node Prague is strongest in advanced light microscopy, and super-resolution methods such as STED and single-molecule localisation microscopy (SMLM) are offered at several of its facilities. Electron microscopy is its second main strength, and two facilities link the two through correlative light and electron microscopy (CLEM). The Node brings together seven facilities at six institutions in Prague, Vestec and České Budějovice. Six facilities offer light microscopy and three offer electron microscopy. Imaging of living animals and plants extends the Node from biological into biomedical imaging.

The staff bring complementary expertise. At the IPHYS Bio-Imaging Facility in Prague, a team of biologists, engineers and mathematicians customises microscopes and measures the mechanical properties of samples. IFIEB in Prague specialises in imaging plants under near-natural conditions, from single cells to growing roots. The EM facility in Prague prepares cell cultures, tissues, worms and microorganisms by chemical fixation or cryo-immobilisation. LEM in České Budějovice develops sample preparation for serial block-face imaging and large-volume cryo work through collaborations. The Imaging Methods Core Facility in Vestec advises on every step from experimental design to analysis and interpretation, for samples that range from macromolecules to organoids.

Plant biologists will find the most specialised support at IFIEB. Cell and structural biologists who need ultrastructure, from routine transmission electron microscopy (TEM) to cryo methods, should look to the EM facility and LEM. Projects that combine super-resolution with quantitative fluorescence can draw on the light microscopy cores in Prague and Vestec. Researchers who work with living rodents will find intravital microscopy at two Prague facilities.

## Imaging technologies offered

Fluorescence microscopy is the core of the Node. Confocal and spinning-disk confocal microscopy are offered at five facilities, for optical sectioning and fast live imaging of cells and tissues. For structures below the diffraction limit, STED is offered at IFIEB, the LM facility and the Imaging Methods Core Facility. SMLM is offered at the LM facility, the Imaging Methods Core Facility and the Microscopy Service Centre. IFIEB, the LM facility and the Imaging Methods Core Facility also run fluorescence lifetime imaging (FLIM), FRET and fluorescence correlation spectroscopy, which measure molecular interactions, local environment and diffusion in cells.

Two-photon and intravital microscopy follow cells deep in tissue and in living animals at the IPHYS Bio-Imaging Facility, the Imaging Methods Core Facility and the Microscopy Service Centre. The Microscopy Service Centre in Prague uses a Femtonics FEMTO3D Atlas for its intravital work. IPHYS also offers micro-PET/CT, optical projection tomography and a Nikon spinning-disk SoRa system combined with Brillouin microscopy, which reads out local stiffness without touching the sample. At IFIEB, a vertical-stage Carl Zeiss LSM880 with Airyscan images plant roots as they grow upright.

Electron microscopy covers TEM of chemically fixed samples at the EM facility, LEM and the Imaging Methods Core Facility. For volume imaging, LEM offers serial block-face SEM and array tomography, and the Imaging Methods Core Facility offers focused ion beam SEM. Live-cell CLEM at the EM facility and the Imaging Methods Core Facility first locates a fluorescent event and then reveals its ultrastructure.

## Specialties and research focus

The research the Node serves falls into a few clear groups. IFIEB works with plant samples and serves plant biology, with an emphasis on development and growth followed under near-natural conditions, such as roots tracked over long time-lapse experiments. Cell biology and structural biology are the stated focus of the two dedicated electron microscopy facilities, the EM facility and LEM. The EM facility handles human and animal cell cultures, plant and animal tissues, worms, microorganisms and lipid micelles. At the Imaging Methods Core Facility, electron microscopy projects range from macromolecules and microbes to cells, tissues and organoids.

Biomedical work centres on living animals. The Microscopy Service Centre works with small rodents, including surgery and cranial-window preparations, and carries out its intravital imaging in a biosafety level 2 laboratory. The IPHYS Bio-Imaging Facility supports preclinical and small-animal research. Its staff also characterise the mechanical behaviour of samples with indentation, tensile testing and optical methods, which serves projects where the physical properties of cells and tissues are part of the question.

## Services and user support

A project usually starts with methodological setup, where staff help design the study protocol and the standard operating procedures. Researchers can then be trained to use the instruments, and training workstations and a seminar room support this teaching. Alternatively, staff provide technical assistance and run the instrument with the visiting researcher.

For work at the bench, the Node provides wet lab space and cell culture facilities at safety levels 1 and 2. Animal facilities and housing facilities are also part of the support, and biological material can be stored and processed through biobanking. Once images are acquired, staff help with data processing and analysis, so quantification does not have to wait until the researcher returns home. The Node also offers server space and data storage for the images and results a project produces.

Most of the Node's seven facilities are in Prague. The Institute of Molecular Genetics hosts two of them, the LM facility and the EM facility, so a project can move from light to electron microscopy within one institute. The LM facility is a broad light microscopy core and the only facility in the Node that offers high-content screening. The Imaging Methods Core Facility in Vestec, part of Charles University, brings light microscopy, electron microscopy and image analysis together in one place, which suits correlative and volume EM projects that need analysis support as well.

The IPHYS Bio-Imaging Facility at the Institute of Physiology combines microscope customisation with mechanical measurements and preclinical imaging. The Microscopy Service Centre at the Institute of Experimental Medicine concentrates on intravital imaging of small rodents and supports the surgery and acquisition that this work needs. IFIEB at the Institute of Experimental Botany is the Node's plant imaging specialist. LEM at the Biology Centre CAS is the only facility in České Budějovice, and it adds electron microscopy with a strong volume and cryo component. Three facilities offer electron microscopy and six offer light microscopy, so most projects can find both within the Node.
