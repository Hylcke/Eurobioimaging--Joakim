# Advanced Light Microscopy Italian Node (ALM)

## Overview

The Advanced Light Microscopy Italian Node (ALM) offers advanced light microscopy at five facilities, from confocal and super-resolution imaging to non-linear and light-sheet microscopy of thick and cleared tissue. Two of these facilities, in Naples and Milan, add electron microscopy, so a project can move from a fluorescent signal to the ultrastructure around it without changing site. The Node is spread over five institutions in five cities, which are Naples, Genoa, Padua, Florence and Milan. It works in biological imaging, and its coordinating facility is the Euro-BioImaging imaging facility in Naples.

The staff develop imaging methods as well as run them. The ALM Node@LENS access facility in Florence sits within a large laser laboratory, and its team specialises in non-linear microscopy, functional imaging and light-sheet microscopy of cleared or expanded specimens. The team at the Nikon Imaging Centre at IIT in Genoa works on super-resolution approaches, including single-molecule localisation microscopy. ALEMBIC in Milan combines microscopy development with biological experiments and has experience with large samples and high-throughput imaging. The Naples group has a long history of combining light and electron microscopy, and it develops FRET-based methods for detecting large multi-molecular complexes. The Padua Laboratory of Ca2+ and cAMP signaling in physiology and pathology builds fluorescent reporters for calcium signalling and for contacts between organelles.

Neuroscientists and groups imaging intact or cleared tissue will find most support at the ALM Node@LENS access facility in Florence. Cell biologists who want to follow a fluorescent signal down to ultrastructure should look first to the Euro-BioImaging imaging facility in Naples, which offers STED, FIB-SEM and EM tomography alongside confocal imaging. Projects that compare many conditions or image whole organs suit ALEMBIC in Milan, which runs high-content screening, light-sheet microscopy and spinning disk confocal imaging.

## Imaging technologies offered

Confocal microscopy is the most widely shared method in the Node. Laser scanning confocal microscopy is offered by ALEMBIC in Milan, the Euro-BioImaging imaging facility in Naples and the Nikon Imaging Centre at IIT in Genoa. Milan and Naples also run spinning disk confocal systems for faster imaging of live cells with less light exposure. ALEMBIC adds deconvolution widefield microscopy on a GE Healthcare DeltaVision Ultra, and TIRF for high contrast at membranes near the coverslip.

For detail below the diffraction limit, the Naples facility offers STED on a Leica SP5 STED. Single-molecule localisation microscopy, which maps individual molecules within subcellular structures, is offered by the Nikon Imaging Centre at IIT in Genoa and the ALM Node@LENS access facility in Florence. Florence is also the centre for non-linear imaging. Two-photon microscopy in Florence and Naples gives optical sections deep in thick tissue. Second and third harmonic generation and Raman spectroscopy in Florence reveal tissue architecture and molecular composition without added labels. Light-sheet microscopy in Florence and Milan records embryos, organs and cleared samples in 3D, with a Zeiss Lightsheet Z1 in Milan.

Functional methods measure what molecules do in living cells. FLIM is offered in Florence, Milan and Naples, FRET in Florence and Naples, and FRAP in Naples. High-content screening runs in Milan and Naples. For 3D ultrastructure, Naples offers FIB-SEM and EM tomography on an FEI Tecnai G2 Spirit BT, and ALEMBIC also offers EM tomography.

## Specialties and research focus

The Node's facilities serve cell biology and biomedical research, and each site brings its own scientific focus. The ALM Node@LENS access facility in Florence supports neuroscience, including brain simulation work, as well as general life-science imaging. It works with human and animal samples, and many of its specimens are cleared or physically expanded before imaging.

The Euro-BioImaging imaging facility in Naples serves cell biology and oncology, and correlative microscopy runs through much of its research. Its samples range from cultured cells and tissue slices to fractionated organelles and viral particles. ALEMBIC in Milan supports biomedical research broadly, and its published work includes studies of mucosal tissue in ear, nose and throat research and of glioma. The Padua Laboratory of Ca2+ and cAMP signaling in physiology and pathology in Padua studies cellular calcium signalling and mitochondrial function. Its work also extends to disease, with research on Alzheimer's disease and hereditary spastic paraplegia, and it measures calcium dynamics alongside reporters of contacts between organelles.

## Services and user support

A project with the Node usually begins with support in experimental design, where staff help match the biological question to a suitable method and plan the imaging. During acquisition, staff give technical assistance with the microscopes, which helps when a method such as STED or FIB-SEM is new to the visiting group. Practical training courses let researchers learn the techniques in more depth, across both light and electron microscopy.

For work with living material, the Node provides cell culture facilities and wet lab space, so cells and tissues can be prepared and kept on site. Animal facilities support projects that rely on animal models. After acquisition, staff help with data processing and analysis, which matters for methods such as single-molecule localisation, FLIM and high-content screening that depend on careful quantification. The Naples team also develops its own image-analysis methods.

The five facilities are spread across Italy, and each is hosted by a different institution. The Euro-BioImaging imaging facility at IEOMI-CNR in Naples coordinates the Node and holds its broadest mix of light microscopy, electron microscopy and functional methods. ALEMBIC at IRCCS Ospedale San Raffaele and Vita-Salute San Raffaele University in Milan is the other site with electron microscopy. A correlative project can therefore stay within one facility in either city.

The Nikon Imaging Centre at IIT in Genoa is a light microscopy core run in partnership between the Italian Institute of Technology and Nikon Instruments Italy. It concentrates on super-resolution, with single-molecule localisation and structured illumination microscopy alongside confocal imaging, and it also offers atomic force microscopy for surface and force measurements. The ALM Node@LENS access facility in Florence is hosted by the European Laboratory for Non-linear Spectroscopy at the University of Florence, a large laser facility, which suits it to non-linear and label-free imaging.

The Padua Laboratory of Ca2+ and cAMP signaling in physiology and pathology belongs to the University of Padua and the CNR Institute of Neuroscience. It is a research laboratory that uses biochemical, molecular and imaging approaches to study organelle function. Within the Node, it brings imaging of ions and other physiological signals in cells and organelles.
