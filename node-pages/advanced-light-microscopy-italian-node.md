# Advanced Light Microscopy Italian Node (ALM)

## Overview

The Advanced Light Microscopy Italian Node (ALM) mainly offers advanced light microscopy, from confocal and two-photon imaging to super-resolution, light-sheet and functional fluorescence methods. Two of its facilities also offer electron microscopy, including electron tomography for three-dimensional views of cell ultrastructure. The Node has five facilities at five institutions in Florence, Genoa, Milan, Naples and Padua, and it works in biological imaging.

Staff across the Node develop imaging methods as well as running them. In Naples, the team develops FRET-based methods for detecting large multi-molecular complexes and writes its own image-analysis methods. The Nikon Imaging Centre at IIT in Genoa works on super-resolution and expansion microscopy. The ALM Node@LENS access facility in Florence is expert in non-linear microscopy, functional imaging and light-sheet microscopy of cleared or expanded samples. ALEMBIC in Milan combines microscopy development with biological experimentation and has experience with large samples and high-throughput microscopy. The Padua Laboratory of Ca2+ and cAMP signaling in physiology and pathology is expert in calcium signalling and in fluorescent reporters of contacts between organelles.

Super-resolution imaging is spread across three sites. STED is at the Euro-BioImaging imaging facility, while single-molecule localisation microscopy is at the Nikon Imaging Centre at IIT and the ALM Node@LENS access facility. Light-sheet imaging and tissue clearing are at ALEMBIC and the ALM Node@LENS access facility, and label-free Raman and harmonic generation imaging are at the ALM Node@LENS access facility alone. Electron tomography is at ALEMBIC and the Euro-BioImaging imaging facility, and FIB-SEM is at the Euro-BioImaging imaging facility. Ion and voltage imaging is at the Padua Laboratory of Ca2+ and cAMP signaling in physiology and pathology.

## Imaging technologies offered

Fluorescence microscopy is the core of the Node. Laser scanning confocal microscopy at ALEMBIC, the Euro-BioImaging imaging facility and the Nikon Imaging Centre at IIT gives optical sections and multichannel 3D localisation in cells and tissues. ALEMBIC also offers TIRF for membranes near the coverslip, and deconvolution widefield imaging on a GE Healthcare DeltaVision Ultra. Spinning-disk confocal imaging at ALEMBIC and the Euro-BioImaging imaging facility records quickly with less light on the sample.

To see detail below the diffraction limit, the Euro-BioImaging imaging facility runs STED on a Leica SP5 STED. Single-molecule localisation microscopy at the Nikon Imaging Centre at IIT and the ALM Node@LENS access facility maps the positions of individual molecules. Functional methods measure what molecules do rather than where they are. FLIM and FRET report molecular environment and nanometre proximity between partners, and FRAP at the Euro-BioImaging imaging facility measures how fast proteins move and exchange.

For thick and large samples, two-photon microscopy at the ALM Node@LENS access facility and the Euro-BioImaging imaging facility images cells deep in tissue. ALEMBIC images cleared organs and embryos on a Zeiss Lightsheet Z1. ALEMBIC and the Euro-BioImaging imaging facility run high-content screening, and ALEMBIC adds imaging flow cytometry. Electron tomography on an FEI Tecnai G2 Spirit BT and FIB-SEM in Naples give 3D ultrastructure.

## Specialties and research focus

Research at the Node centres on cell biology and on how cells behave in health and disease. The Euro-BioImaging imaging facility in Naples supports cell biology and oncology, and works with cultured cells, tissue slices, fractionated organelles and viral particles. Its development of FRET-based methods serves questions about how large protein complexes assemble inside cells.

Neuroscience is the main field at the ALM Node@LENS access facility, including work linked to brain simulation alongside general life-science imaging. The facility handles human and animal samples, often after clearing or expansion so that whole tissues can be imaged in three dimensions.

ALEMBIC in Milan serves biomedical research broadly, with published work on mucosal tissue of the ear, nose and throat and on glioma. The Padua Laboratory of Ca2+ and cAMP signaling in physiology and pathology studies cellular calcium signalling and mitochondrial function. Its disease interests include Alzheimer's disease and hereditary spastic paraplegia, and it has built reversible reporters for contacts between organelles together with measurements of the calcium dynamics at those contacts.

## Services and user support

Work with the Node starts with support in experimental design, where staff help choose the method and plan the experiment around the biological question. This matters on a Node where related methods sit at different sites and the right choice depends on the sample.

Researchers then get technical assistance in using the microscopes, and the Node also runs practical training courses for those who want to learn the methods in more depth. Visiting groups do not need to bring everything ready to image. Cell culture facilities and wet lab space let researchers prepare and maintain their samples on site, and animal facilities support experiments that involve animal models.

Once images are acquired, staff help with data processing and analysis. This links to the image-analysis methods developed within the Node, so researchers get help turning raw images into measurements such as molecular proximity, mobility or the organisation of structures in three dimensions.

Two facilities combine light and electron microscopy. The Euro-BioImaging imaging facility in Naples coordinates the Node and offers confocal, STED and functional fluorescence methods together with electron tomography and FIB-SEM. It also runs high-throughput screening and mass spectrometry imaging. ALEMBIC in Milan joins microscopy development with biological experimentation, and its work spans confocal, TIRF, light-sheet and high-content imaging alongside electron tomography and imaging of cryo-immobilised samples.

The Nikon Imaging Centre at IIT in Genoa is a light microscopy core built in partnership between IIT and Nikon Instruments Italy. Its focus is nanoscale imaging through single-molecule localisation, structured illumination and expansion microscopy, and it also offers atomic force microscopy for surface topography and mechanical measurements.

The ALM Node@LENS access facility in Florence sits within a large laser research centre and specialises in non-linear optics. It offers two-photon, harmonic generation and Raman imaging, and it combines light-sheet microscopy with tissue clearing and expansion for imaging whole brain tissue and other large specimens.

The Padua Laboratory of Ca2+ and cAMP signaling in physiology and pathology is a research laboratory that studies calcium signalling and organelle function with biochemical, molecular and imaging approaches. Its contribution to the Node is ion and voltage imaging, which follows physiological changes inside cells and their organelles.
