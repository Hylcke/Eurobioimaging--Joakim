# Austrian BioImaging Node / CMI

## Overview

The Austrian BioImaging Node / CMI (Correlated Multimodality Imaging) combines light microscopy, electron microscopy and preclinical imaging so that one sample can be studied at several scales. Its methods run from cryo-electron microscopy and super-resolution microscopy to MRI, PET and SPECT in small animals. The Node has 19 facilities at eight institutions in Vienna, Klosterneuburg, Himberg and four towns in Upper Austria, and it covers both biological and biomedical imaging.

Staff across the Node design pipelines that correlate several imaging methods. They start from the research question rather than from a fixed technique. The Electron Microscopy Facility team in Klosterneuburg works in cryo-EM for structural biology and runs specialist preparation workflows for frozen samples. At the VBCF Electron Microscopy Facility in Vienna, staff handle samples from molecules and viruses to bacteria, cells and tissues, and they prepare them by chemical fixation, high-pressure or plunge freezing and immunolabelling. IOF in Klosterneuburg is experienced in automation and image analysis, including long time-lapse imaging of growing plant roots. PIL in Vienna runs integrated multimodal workflows for in vivo, ex vivo and in vitro studies, while VetImaging in Vienna brings expertise in X-ray microscopy, image processing and visualisation.

Confocal, TIRF and super-resolution microscopy are at IOF in Klosterneuburg and the MedUni Vienna Core Facility Imaging in Vienna. Cryo-electron microscopy is at the Electron Microscopy Facility in Klosterneuburg and the VBCF Electron Microscopy Facility in Vienna, and CIUS in Vienna offers immunogold labelling and cryo-SEM. Preclinical MRI, PET and SPECT are at PIL in Vienna, and micro-CT is at VetImaging in Vienna. Plant phenotyping is at the PHENOPlant Facility in Vienna, while atomic force microscopy is at AIC in Vienna and the TIMed CENTER Core Facilities in Upper Austria.

## Imaging technologies offered

Light microscopy has the most facilities at the Node. Laser scanning confocal microscopy is offered at four facilities and gives optical sections and multichannel 3D localisation in cells and tissues. IOF and the MedUni Vienna Core Facility Imaging add spinning disk confocal microscopy for fast sectioning with less light exposure, and TIRF for high-contrast imaging near the cell surface. Single-molecule localisation microscopy maps molecular distributions below the diffraction limit at IOF, the MedUni Vienna Core Facility Imaging and the TIMed CENTER Core Facilities. IOF also offers STED and two-photon microscopy, which images cells deep in thick tissue. FRET measures nanometre proximity between molecules at the MedUni Vienna Core Facility Imaging and VBCF Advanced Microscopy. The MedUni Vienna Core Facility Imaging runs a Zeiss LSM 980 Airyscan 2 and an ONI Nanoimager.

Electron microscopy reaches from single macromolecules to tissue ultrastructure. Cryo-TEM keeps samples hydrated and close to their native state, and cryo-FIB thins frozen cells so that their interior can be imaged. The Electron Microscopy Facility runs a 300 kV Thermo Fisher Titan Krios. The VBCF Electron Microscopy Facility adds cryo-electron tomography, TEM of chemically fixed samples and Tokuyasu immunogold labelling. In living animals, PIL offers MRI, PET and SPECT along with other in vivo modalities, and its Bruker BioSpec 9.4 T/30 carries a PET insert for combined PET/MRI. Micro-CT at VetImaging shows 3D anatomy including mineralised structures. Raman spectroscopy at AIC reads chemical composition without labels.

## Specialties and research focus

Preclinical research at the Node centres on PIL, which supports oncology, cardiovascular, neuroradiology, pharmacokinetic and musculoskeletal studies. The Center for Biomedical Research and Translational Surgery, with sites in Vienna and Himberg, works on cardiovascular research and surgical device testing in large animals. MMCT in Vienna studies cancer therapeutics, including gene and nucleic-acid delivery and the preclinical evaluation of treatments.

At the cellular and molecular scale, the Electron Microscopy Facility serves structural biology. The VBCF Electron Microscopy Facility studies the ultrastructure of molecules, viruses, bacteria, organelles, cells and tissues. VBCF Advanced Microscopy in Vienna supports cell and developmental biology, and AIC works on metabolomics, bioprocess analysis and the analysis of nanoparticles and viruses.

Several facilities work with plants. The PHENOPlant Facility studies Arabidopsis, crop plants and trees, including drought and climate-stress studies, and handles plants up to 100 cm tall including the pot. IOF follows plant roots over long imaging sessions, and CIUS images frozen hydrated plant and hydrogel samples. The Clinical Unit of Diagnostic Imaging at Vetmeduni in Vienna works in veterinary clinical diagnostics.

## Services and user support

A project starts with methodological setup, where staff help design the study protocol and the standard operating procedures. Researchers then either receive training in using the infrastructure or work with technical assistance from staff who run the instruments. Training takes place at dedicated training workstations and in a training seminar room.

For animal studies, the Node provides animal facilities and housing facilities, and staff prepare the animals for imaging. The Node also provides imaging probes and probe preparation, and it has a tracer development and radiopharmacy facility with a cyclotron. Wet lab space and cell culture facilities give visiting researchers room to work with their samples before and between imaging sessions. Once images are acquired, staff help with data processing and analysis, and the Node provides server space and data storage for the results.

Electron microscopy is spread over several facilities. The Electron Microscopy Facility at ISTA focuses on cryo-EM, cryo-FIB and electron tomography for structural biology. The VBCF Electron Microscopy Facility combines cryo-EM with TEM and SEM, and its staff cover preparation from chemical fixation to freezing. CIUS at the University of Vienna links confocal microscopy with SEM, cryo-SEM and immunogold labelling, and its staff are expert in ultrastructure and elemental analysis. USTEM at TU Wien contributes electron microscopy expertise and teaching.

In light microscopy, IOF at ISTA offers confocal, STED and two-photon microscopy alongside expertise in automation and image analysis. The MedUni Vienna Core Facility Imaging works on 2D, 3D and 4D microscopy of fixed and living cells, from whole tissue to subcellular detail. VBCF Advanced Microscopy specialises in FRET and advanced optical microscopy.

PIL at the Medical University of Vienna covers multimodal preclinical imaging, and VetImaging at Vetmeduni pairs confocal microscopy with micro-CT and image analysis. The Biomedical Image Informatics Group at VRVis develops methods for analysing and visualising biomedical images, including AI and visual analytics. AIC and the TIMed CENTER Core Facilities offer sample characterisation by atomic force microscopy, and the PHENOPlant Facility runs automated high-throughput plant phenotyping. Further groups at the Medical University of Vienna, Vetmeduni, the University of Vienna and VBCF bring expertise in translational surgery, veterinary diagnostics, cancer therapeutics, tissue preparation and specialist optical imaging.
