# Barcelona Mesoscopic Imaging Node (B-Min)

## Overview

The Barcelona Mesoscopic Imaging Node (B-Min) specialises in light-sheet fluorescence microscopy of specimens much larger than a single cell. Its custom-built light-sheet instruments image samples from about 50 µm across to more than 5 cm, so one Node can handle a small organoid as well as a whole cleared organ. B-Min is a biological imaging Node with two facilities at two institutions. Advanced Digital Microscopy (ADM) is part of IRB Barcelona in Barcelona. The Super-resolution Light Microscopy & Nanoscopy Facility (SLN) belongs to the Institute of Photonic Sciences (ICFO) in Castelldefels, also in the Barcelona area. Around the light-sheet core, both facilities add confocal and fluorescence lifetime imaging, and SLN contributes label-free methods.

The staff design and build much of their own light-sheet equipment and work with many variants of the technique. These include dual-sided illumination, axially swept light sheets that keep resolution even across large fields, and oblique plane microscopy for imaging in multiwell plates. ADM brings experience in tissue clearing, quantitative bioimaging and bioimage analysis, and its main samples are cleared organs, embryos and organoids. SLN concentrates on optical method development and on research and development support for biomedical applications, with particular experience in multiphoton and other nonlinear optics. Both teams combine light-sheet data with complementary methods in correlative projects.

Researchers who need to image whole cleared organs or large tissue blocks in three dimensions will find most support at ADM. ADM also suits groups that want to screen organoids in multiwell format. Developmental biologists working with embryos can use the light-sheet systems at either facility. Projects that need contrast without labels, such as ordered tissue structures or the chemical make-up of a sample, belong at SLN.

## Imaging technologies offered

Light-sheet mesoscopic imaging is the centre of the Node, and both ADM and SLN offer it. A thin sheet of light illuminates only the plane being recorded, which limits unnecessary exposure and makes it practical to capture cells, embryos and organs in three dimensions. At ADM, the Custom MacroSPIM images cleared tissue between about 0.5 and 3 cm, and the Custom LEMOLISH takes cleared samples of 1 to 5 cm at very low magnification. SLN runs a custom multimodal SPIM/DSLM. ADM also prepares samples by tissue clearing, which opens up whole organs to optical imaging without physical sectioning.

For larger numbers of samples, ADM offers high-throughput microscopy and high-content screening on its Custom Oblique Plane Microscope, which is set up for organoids. Feedback microscopy and image analysis at ADM support automated acquisition and quantification.

Confocal microscopy and fluorescence lifetime imaging (FLIM) are available at both facilities. FLIM adds contrast that reports on the molecular environment and on interactions, beyond plain intensity. SLN runs FLIM on a Leica TCS SP8 FALCON. SLN also offers two-photon microscopy for deeper optical sectioning in thick tissue. In addition, it provides second and third harmonic generation, which reveals ordered structures without a fluorescent label, and Raman spectroscopy on a Renishaw InVia confocal Raman system for molecular fingerprints of small regions.

## Specialties and research focus

The Node's work is defined by sample scale. Its research spans specimens from tens of micrometres to several centimetres, and the facilities are set up for samples that are too large or too thick for conventional microscopy alone.

ADM works mainly with cleared organs, embryos and organoids. Its projects centre on quantitative bioimaging, where images are turned into measurements, and on high-resolution optical imaging supported by bioimage analysis. Preclinical imaging is among the examples of its use, and the clearing and large-sample light-sheet work at ADM supports studies of tissue organisation across whole organs.

SLN serves biomedical and life-science imaging, and its sample types include organoids, small embryos and corneal specimens. Because the facility sits within a photonics institute, much of its work combines biological questions with the development of new optical methods. This makes SLN a natural partner for projects where the imaging approach itself has to be adapted to the sample.

Organoids and embryos are handled at both facilities, which gives researchers working on development and on three-dimensional culture models two places to turn.

## Services and user support

A project at B-Min usually begins with methodological set-up, where staff help design the study protocol and the standard operating procedures for the imaging. Researchers can then be trained to use the infrastructure, with training workstations and a seminar room for this, or run their experiments with technical assistance from staff.

Sample work is well supported. Staff can help with probe preparation such as immunofluorescence labelling, and a wet lab is available, including for preparing living embryos for immediate imaging. Drosophila facilities can be arranged, and there are cell culture facilities at biosafety levels 1 and 2. Biological material can be kept in cold, frozen or liquid-nitrogen storage. Histology is offered for tissue imaging and for correlative workflows that link histology to mesoscopic imaging. When a sample needs an unusual mount, 3D printing and a mechanical and electronic workshop can adapt the hardware.

After acquisition, staff support data processing and analysis, and server space holds the data during the project. Visiting researchers can also get help finding housing.

B-Min is built from two facilities that share a light-sheet core but bring different strengths around it.

ADM is IRB Barcelona's core facility for microscopy and bioimage analysis. It covers the largest end of the Node's size range, with light-sheet systems for cleared tissue up to several centimetres and tissue clearing to prepare those samples. It also brings the Node's automated methods: screening in multiwell format, feedback-controlled acquisition and image analysis. This makes ADM the natural home for projects that need many samples imaged in the same way or large data sets quantified.

SLN is a specialised facility at ICFO in Castelldefels, and it combines microscopy access with method development and research and development support. Alongside light-sheet, confocal and lifetime imaging, it offers the Node's two-photon, harmonic generation and Raman methods. Its samples include organoids, small embryos and corneal specimens, and it suits projects that need tissue structure or chemistry measured without labels.

The two sites complement each other. A researcher can map a whole cleared organ or an organoid series at ADM and examine selected samples with label-free methods at SLN. Because confocal microscopy and FLIM run at both facilities, a project that starts at one site can continue with comparable methods at the other.
