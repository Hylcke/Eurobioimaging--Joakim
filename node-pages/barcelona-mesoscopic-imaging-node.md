# Barcelona Mesoscopic Imaging Node (B-Min)

## Overview

The Barcelona Mesoscopic Imaging Node (B-Min) centres on light-sheet fluorescence microscopy of mesoscopic specimens such as embryos, organoids and cleared organs. Much of its light-sheet instrumentation is custom-built, and together the instruments cover samples from about 50 µm to more than 5 cm across. The Node works in biological imaging through two facilities, ADM at IRB Barcelona in Barcelona and SLN at the Institute of Photonic Sciences (ICFO) in Castelldefels.

The staff build and adapt light-sheet microscopes in several configurations, including digitally scanned, axially swept and oblique plane designs. They also combine light-sheet imaging with complementary methods for correlative work. The ADM team works in quantitative bioimaging, high-resolution optical imaging and bioimage analysis, and handles cleared organs, embryos and organoids. The team at SLN develops new methods and supports research and development for biomedical applications, with particular experience in multiphoton and nonlinear optical imaging. SLN works with organoids, small embryos and corneal specimens.

Light-sheet mesoscopic imaging is at ADM in Barcelona and SLN in Castelldefels, which also both offer confocal microscopy and fluorescence lifetime imaging (FLIM). ADM adds high-content screening and tissue clearing, along with feedback microscopy. Image analysis is also part of what ADM offers. Two-photon microscopy, second and third harmonic generation imaging and Raman spectroscopy are at SLN.

## Imaging technologies offered

Light-sheet microscopy illuminates only the plane being recorded, so researchers can image large specimens in three dimensions with little unnecessary light exposure. ADM and SLN both offer it. At ADM, the custom MacroSPIM images cleared tissue at low magnification, and the custom LEMOLISH takes larger cleared organs. Tissue clearing at ADM makes organs transparent, which gives optical access to their 3D organisation without cutting serial sections. SLN runs its own custom multimodal SPIM/DSLM.

ADM also images organoids at high throughput on a custom Oblique Plane Microscope. This supports high-content screening, which measures many phenotypes across many samples and conditions. Feedback microscopy at ADM uses image analysis to find defined events and adapt the acquisition to them.

Confocal microscopy at both facilities provides optical sectioning and multichannel 3D localisation in cells and tissues. Both facilities pair it with FLIM, which adds contrast sensitive to the molecular environment and to interactions between molecules. SLN extends the light microscopy into thick and unlabelled tissue. Two-photon microscopy gives optical sectioning deep inside tissue, and harmonic generation imaging shows ordered tissue architecture without any added fluorophore. Raman spectroscopy on a Renishaw InVia confocal Raman reads the molecular composition of small regions through their vibrational fingerprint.

## Specialties and research focus

The Node serves research that needs whole specimens imaged in three dimensions rather than thin sections. Its sample types reflect this. ADM works with cleared organs, embryos and organoids, while SLN works with organoids, small embryos and corneal specimens. Because the light-sheet instruments span samples from tens of micrometres to several centimetres, a project can follow tissue organisation from small organoids up to large intact organs.

ADM supports research projects at IRB Barcelona and beyond through quantitative bioimaging and bioimage analysis, and preclinical imaging is among its examples of use. Its work with organoids extends to screening many samples under different conditions, so phenotypes can be compared systematically.

SLN focuses on biomedical and life-science imaging, and its method development work feeds directly into the research it supports. Corneal specimens are among the tissues it images, alongside organoids and small embryos.

Across both facilities, the Node builds complete imaging workflows around light-sheet microscopy and links mesoscopic imaging with complementary methods in correlative studies. One example is correlative histology-to-mesoscopic imaging, which connects classical tissue sections with 3D views of the same tissue.

## Services and user support

A project starts with methodological setup, where staff help design the study protocol and standard operating procedures. Researchers then receive training in using the infrastructure, supported by training workstations and a seminar room. During acquisition, staff give technical assistance to run the instruments.

Sample preparation is part of the service. Staff carry out probe preparation, including immunofluorescence labelling and expansion microscopy, and provide histology for tissue imaging and for correlative histology-to-mesoscopic work. Visiting researchers have wet lab space and a wet lab for animal preparation, where living embryos can be prepared for immediate imaging. Drosophila facilities are also part of the offer. Cell culture facilities run at safety levels 1 and 2, and biological material can be kept in conventional cold, frozen or nitrogen-frozen storage.

For awkward specimens, the Node uses 3D printing to adapt sample mounting, and a mechanical and electronic workshop supports the instruments. After imaging, staff help with data processing and analysis, and server space holds the data. The Node also helps visiting researchers find housing.

Advanced Digital Microscopy (ADM) is the core microscopy and bioimage analysis facility of IRB Barcelona in Barcelona. Its focus is light-sheet imaging of large and cleared samples, and it offers tissue clearing so that whole organs can be imaged intact. ADM also runs high-content screening of organoids, feedback microscopy, confocal microscopy and FLIM. Image analysis is a central part of its work, and its staff are experienced in quantitative bioimaging and high-resolution optical imaging. The facility works with cleared organs, embryos and organoids, and lists preclinical imaging among its examples of use.

The Super-resolution Light Microscopy & Nanoscopy Facility (SLN) at ICFO in Castelldefels sits within a photonics institute. It combines microscopy access and training with method development and research and development support for biomedical applications. SLN offers light-sheet imaging on a custom multimodal system, together with confocal microscopy and FLIM. It also offers nonlinear and label-free methods that ADM does not, namely two-photon microscopy, second and third harmonic generation imaging and Raman spectroscopy. Its staff have expertise in multiphoton and nonlinear optics, and the facility works with organoids, small embryos and corneal specimens.

Together the two facilities share light-sheet, confocal and lifetime imaging. ADM adds clearing, screening and analysis, while SLN adds deep-tissue and label-free contrast.
