# Bilbao Node (ALM@BIO)

## Overview

The Bilbao Node mainly offers advanced light microscopy for quantitative studies of cellular processes, with a particular emphasis on molecular interactions and biophysics. Its light microscopy runs from confocal and two-photon imaging to super-resolution and fluorescence methods that measure how molecules move and interact, and cryo-electron microscopy adds structural biology. The Node has three facilities at two institutions, the Achucarro Basque Center for Neuroscience and the Biofisika Institute (CSIC-UPV/EHU), all in Leioa in the Bilbao metropolitan area, and it covers biological imaging.

Staff expertise centres on biophysics, neuroscience and structural biology. The Node's biophysical work looks at lipid-mediated protein interactions and at lipid order in model membranes and cells. BRALM in Leioa offers the fluorescence methods that measure molecular diffusion, proximity and environment, and its staff also work with expanded and cleared specimens. The Achucarro Imaging Facility in Leioa supports neuroscience projects across scales, in both in vitro and in vivo workflows. Its staff image live brain slices and combine calcium imaging with electrophysiology. At BREM in Leioa, the expertise lies in the structural biology of macromolecular complexes, organelles, cells and tissues.

Confocal and two-photon microscopy are offered by both the Achucarro Imaging Facility and BRALM. STED super-resolution and high-content screening are at the Achucarro Imaging Facility. Single-molecule localisation microscopy and light-sheet imaging are at BRALM. BRALM also offers FLIM, FRET and fluorescence correlation spectroscopy, along with atomic force microscopy and image analysis. Cryo-electron microscopy and cryo-correlative light and electron microscopy are at BREM.

## Imaging technologies offered

Most of the Node's methods are fluorescence light microscopy, offered at the Achucarro Imaging Facility and BRALM. Confocal microscopy at both facilities gives optical sections and multichannel 3D localisation in cells and tissues. Two-photon microscopy, also at both, images cells deep within thick tissue. The Femtonics Femto-2D at the Achucarro Imaging Facility combines calcium imaging with electrophysiology. For nanoscale detail, the Leica TCS SP8 STED CW at the Achucarro Imaging Facility resolves structures of about 50 to 80 nanometres and works with live samples. BRALM offers single-molecule localisation microscopy on an ONI Nanoimager, which maps molecular distributions below the diffraction limit.

BRALM also runs spinning disk confocal, TIRF and deconvolution widefield microscopy, and it offers light-sheet imaging for embryos, organs and other large specimens. Its biophysical methods include FCS, which measures diffusion and concentration, and FRET, which reports nanometre proximity between molecules. FLIM adds contrast that is sensitive to the molecular environment. Automated high-content screening at the Achucarro Imaging Facility measures cell phenotypes across many wells. BREM offers cryo-electron microscopy, including cryo-TEM on a Thermo Fisher Krios G4, cryo-electron tomography and cryo-FIB milling. Together these reveal the 3D structure of vitrified macromolecules and of thin regions of cells.

## Specialties and research focus

Neuroscience is a central research field at the Node. Its work addresses how neural circuits are organised and process information, and the genetic and molecular basis of circuit development and ageing. It also covers the genetic, molecular and cellular mechanisms behind neurodegenerative and autoimmune brain diseases, with translational research into new therapies. The Achucarro Imaging Facility serves this brain research and works with live brain slices and in vivo specimens.

Biophysics is the second strand. The Node studies cellular processes quantitatively, with a focus on molecular interactions such as lipid-mediated protein interactions and on lipid order in model membranes and cells. BRALM serves the life sciences in general and handles expanded and cleared biological specimens. At BREM, the research focus is structural biology. BREM supports three-dimensional structure determination of macromolecules and their complexes, organelles, cells and tissues, and work on biological mechanisms and human disease, all with vitrified samples.

## Services and user support

A project usually starts with methodological setup, where staff help design the study protocol and write standard operating procedures. The Node also offers regulatory affairs management. Researchers are then trained in using the infrastructure, and the Node provides training workstations. During imaging, staff give technical assistance with running the instruments.

Preparation is supported in several ways. Staff prepare samples and probes, and the Node offers micropatterning and 3D printing for experimental setups. The Node provides wet lab space and cell culture facilities at safety levels 1 and 2. Flow cytometry and fluorescence-activated cell sorting are also offered, which helps researchers select cell populations before imaging. After acquisition, staff help with data processing and analysis, and the Node provides server space for the data.

Light microscopy is shared between two facilities. The Achucarro Imaging Facility, at the Achucarro Basque Center for Neuroscience, is a core facility for neuroscience. It offers confocal, two-photon and STED microscopy. Voltage, pH and ion imaging and high-content screening are also part of its work. Its staff are experienced with brain tissue imaging, deep-tissue and in vivo functional imaging, and super-resolution of neural structures.

BRALM, the Basque Resource for Advanced Light Microscopy at the Biofisika Institute, provides advanced light microscopy and image analysis for research and industry. Its methods range from widefield, confocal and spinning disk imaging to TIRF, single-molecule localisation and light-sheet microscopy. It also offers fluorescence correlation spectroscopy, FRET and FLIM for studying molecular interactions, as well as photomanipulation and atomic force microscopy. For thick or dense samples, BRALM prepares specimens by expansion and tissue clearing.

Electron microscopy is at BREM, the Basque Resource for Electron Microscopy, also at the Biofisika Institute. It provides high-resolution cryo-electron microscopy and in situ cryo-electron tomography for three-dimensional structural analysis of vitrified specimens. Cryo-FIB, cryo-SEM and cryo-correlative light and electron microscopy complete its methods. Its staff are expert in structural biology from macromolecular complexes to organelles, cells and tissues.
