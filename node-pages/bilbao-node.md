# Bilbao Node (ALM@BIO)

## Overview

The Bilbao Node (ALM@BIO) is built around advanced light microscopy for quantitative, biophysical studies of cells, with a strong link to neuroscience. Two light microscopy facilities offer most of its methods, which measure molecular interactions, mobility and nanoscale organisation, while a third facility adds cryo-electron microscopy for structural biology. The Node brings together three facilities at two institutions. The Achucarro Imaging Facility in Leioa belongs to the Achucarro Basque Center for Neuroscience, while BRALM and BREM, both in Leioa, belong to the Biofisika Institute (CSIC-UPV/EHU). All three sit at the Science Park of the University of the Basque Country and work in biological imaging.

The staff are experienced in quantitative research on cellular processes. Across the Node this includes lipid-mediated protein interactions, imaging of lipid order in model membranes and cells, and the structural biology of macromolecular assemblies. The Achucarro Imaging Facility supports neuroscience projects across scales, from live brain slices to in vivo specimens, and it combines two-photon calcium imaging with electrophysiology. BRALM handles expanded and cleared specimens alongside its single-molecule and fluorescence spectroscopy methods. BREM brings specialist expertise in three-dimensional structural analysis of vitrified macromolecules, organelles, cells and tissues.

Neuroscientists who want to image brain tissue, record activity in living preparations or run high-content screens will find most support at the Achucarro Imaging Facility. Cell biologists and biophysicists who need to measure molecular proximity, diffusion or nanoscale organisation should look to BRALM. Structural biologists studying macromolecular complexes, or molecules in their cellular setting, will find that work at BREM.

## Imaging technologies offered

Light microscopy carries most of the Node's work, with twenty technologies across two facilities. Laser scanning confocal and two-photon microscopy are offered by both the Achucarro Imaging Facility and BRALM, giving optical sectioning in cells and in thick tissue. The Achucarro Imaging Facility adds STED nanoscopy on a Leica TCS SP8 STED CW, which resolves subcellular structures at around 50 to 80 nm and works with live samples. Its Femtonics Femto-2D pairs two-photon calcium imaging with electrophysiology, and it also runs high-content screening across many conditions.

BRALM concentrates on methods that show how molecules behave. Single-molecule localisation microscopy on an ONI Nanoimager maps molecular distributions below the diffraction limit. Fluorescence lifetime imaging (FLIM), FRET and fluorescence correlation spectroscopy report molecular proximity, local environment, diffusion and concentration. TIRF microscopy gives high contrast at the cell membrane, and spinning disk confocal suits fast imaging with less light exposure. Light-sheet imaging handles larger specimens such as embryos and organs. BRALM also offers photomanipulation for targeted perturbation, with expansion microscopy and tissue clearing among its newer methods.

BREM offers a group of cryo-electron microscopy methods, including cryo-transmission electron microscopy and cryo-electron tomography. These preserve samples in a near-native frozen state for structural study, and cryo-CLEM places fluorescent signals in that ultrastructural context. BREM's equipment includes a Thermo Fisher Krios G4.

## Specialties and research focus

Neuroscience runs through much of the Node's research. The Node studies how neural circuits are organised and process information, including the genetic and molecular basis of circuit development and ageing. It also works on the genetic, molecular and cellular mechanisms of neurodegenerative and autoimmune brain diseases, with the aim of supporting translational research into new therapies. At the Achucarro Imaging Facility this work centres on brain research, and its main sample types are live brain slices and in vivo specimens.

Biophysics is the second strand. The Node's quantitative research on cellular processes covers lipid-mediated protein interactions and the lipid order of model membranes and cells. BRALM serves life science research in general and works with expanded and cleared biological specimens. At BREM the focus is three-dimensional structure determination of macromolecules and complexes, organelles, cells and tissues. That structural work feeds into studies of biological mechanisms and human disease, with vitrified samples throughout.

## Services and user support

A project can begin with methodological setup, where staff help design the study protocol and standard operating procedures. The Node also offers a regulatory affairs management service for projects that need it. Researchers can then be trained to use the instruments, or run them with technical assistance from staff, and training workstations are part of this support.

Before imaging, the Node offers sample preparation and probe preparation, and visiting researchers have wet lab space to work in. Cell culture facilities at safety levels 1 and 2 support work with living cells. Micropatterning and 3D printing help with preparing samples and custom parts, and flow cytometry with fluorescence-activated cell sorting lets researchers select cell populations before imaging. Once images are acquired, staff help with data processing and analysis, and server space holds the project data.

The Node joins a neuroscience imaging core with two facilities at a biophysics institute. The Achucarro Imaging Facility is the core facility of the Achucarro Basque Center for Neuroscience. It supports neuroscience projects across scales and in both in vitro and in vivo workflows, so it suits work that moves from cultured cells to brain slices and living preparations.

BRALM and BREM both belong to the Biofisika Institute (CSIC-UPV/EHU). BRALM is a core facility for advanced light microscopy and image analysis, serving research and industry. It holds the largest share of the Node's methods, from routine confocal imaging to single-molecule and spectroscopy techniques, and it adds atomic force microscopy for surface and mechanical measurements. BREM provides academic and industry users with instruments and specialist expertise for high-resolution cryo-electron microscopy and in situ cryo-electron tomography. Its work is backed by high-performance computing and on-the-fly processing.

The facilities complement each other across scales of resolution. Confocal and two-photon microscopy are offered at both the Achucarro Imaging Facility and BRALM, so neuroscience and biophysics groups can use familiar methods at either. Because BRALM and BREM share an institute, a project can follow a molecular signal seen by fluorescence through to its ultrastructure with cryo-CLEM at BREM. All three facilities are at the Science Park of the University of the Basque Country in Leioa.
