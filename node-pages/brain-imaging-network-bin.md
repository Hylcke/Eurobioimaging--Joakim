# Brain Imaging Network (BIN)

## Overview

The Brain Imaging Network (BIN) is Portugal's Node for positron emission tomography (PET) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) in people and in animal models. Its main focus is neuroimaging, and it also applies these methods to oncology and cardiology. The Node has one facility, ICNAS at the University of Coimbra in Coimbra, and it covers biomedical imaging from preclinical studies through to clinical and human imaging.

Staff at ICNAS are expert in combined PET and MR studies of the same subject on the same day. Because the team works with short-lived carbon-11 tracers, one visit can include two PET scans as well as one or more MR scans. The radiochemistry team develops new radiolabelled tracers for processes that PET does not yet measure, and it works mainly with fluorine-18, carbon-11, gallium-68, copper-64 and nitrogen-13. On the MR side, a research agreement with the scanner manufacturer lets staff run work-in-progress sequences, including multinuclear methods. The team also brings machine-learning approaches to the analysis of imaging data.

ICNAS in Coimbra combines all of the Node's main groups of methods. Human imaging covers PET, MRI and MR spectroscopy below 7 T, and combined PET-MRI. Preclinical imaging adds high-field micro-MRI and spectroscopy, micro-PET and micro-PET/MRI, with ultrasound, photoacoustic and optical imaging alongside. Support for population imaging studies and their cohort data is also offered at ICNAS.

## Imaging technologies offered

Human PET and MRI form the core of the Node. PET shows where a radiolabelled tracer goes in the body and how quickly it is taken up or cleared, so researchers can measure tracer kinetics in the living brain and other organs. MRI and MR spectroscopy add anatomy, tissue physiology and local metabolite levels without ionising radiation, and combined PET-MRI places the tracer signal in that soft-tissue context. Human MR studies run on a Siemens Trio Tim 3T MRI. An on-site cyclotron produces both short- and long-lived tracers, which allows several scans with short-lived tracers on one day. The human imaging group also includes PET/CT.

The preclinical methods follow the same logic in animal models. A Bruker BioSpec 9.4T animal MRI provides micro-MRI and spectroscopy in living animals and in ex vivo specimens. Micro-PET runs on a prototype RPC-PET small-animal system with 0.4 mm resolution, and micro-PET/MRI combines both readouts in the same animal. Ultrasound gives quick views of organs and blood flow, while photoacoustic imaging shows vasculature through the way tissue absorbs light. In vivo optical imaging follows fluorescent or bioluminescent reporters across repeated sessions. For cohort work, population imaging support covers study design, secure data preparation and storage, processing and tailored software.

## Specialties and research focus

Neuroscience is the main research field at ICNAS. Its brain imaging work covers neurodegenerative and inflammatory diseases of the nervous system, including Parkinson's disease, Alzheimer's disease and multiple sclerosis. These studies make use of the same-day PET and MR sessions, so a tracer measurement and the MR picture of the brain come from one subject at one time point.

Oncology and cardiology are the two other clinical areas the facility serves. Across all three fields, the work spans translational imaging, which links studies in animal models with studies in people. Machine-learning approaches to the image data form part of the research in clinical neuroscience, oncology and cardiology.

Radiotracer development is a research focus in its own right. The radiochemistry and radiopharmacy groups design new radiolabelled molecules for biological processes that are not currently studied with PET. They also tailor tracers to the specific question of a research project, drawing on fluorine-18, carbon-11, gallium-68, copper-64 and nitrogen-13 chemistry. This tracer work feeds directly into the molecular imaging studies at ICNAS, in both preclinical models and human subjects.

## Services and user support

A project at ICNAS starts with methodological set-up. Staff help design the study protocol and write the standard operating procedures that the imaging sessions will follow. For studies in people, the Node also offers regulatory affairs management and arranges clinical trial insurance contracting, so the formal side of a human study is handled alongside the scientific planning.

Researchers then receive training in using the infrastructure. Training workstations and a training seminar room are part of this, which means visiting scientists can practise acquisition and analysis steps before and between scanning sessions. During acquisition, staff give technical assistance to run the instruments, whether the study involves the human scanners or the preclinical systems.

Preclinical projects can use the animal facilities at ICNAS, and housing facilities are also offered there. Once images are acquired, staff help with data processing and analysis, building on their experience with PET kinetics, MR data and machine-learning approaches. Server space holds the project data during the study.

ICNAS, the Institute for Nuclear Sciences Applied to Health at the University of Coimbra, is a medical imaging research institute that brings the main imaging modalities together in one building. It runs cyclotron and radiochemistry facilities next to its human scanners and a preclinical imaging facility for animal studies, so tracers made on site go straight into imaging sessions in people or in animal models. The institute works across molecular and multimodal medical imaging, radiopharmacy and radiochemistry, and translational and brain imaging.

The radiochemists produce PET tracers matched to individual research questions and develop new radiolabelled molecules, with carbon-11 chemistry central to the same-day multi-scan studies. The MR team implements research protocols beyond standard clinical sequences, including work-in-progress and multinuclear methods made possible by its agreement with the scanner manufacturer. A local clinical trials unit assists researchers with their regulatory interactions when a study involves human participants. The imaging and analysis staff apply this combined expertise to studies of Parkinson's disease, Alzheimer's disease and multiple sclerosis, as well as to projects in oncology and cardiology.
