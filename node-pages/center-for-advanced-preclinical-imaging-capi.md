# Center for Advanced Preclinical Imaging (CAPI)

## Overview

The Center for Advanced Preclinical Imaging (CAPI) is a Czech Node for in vivo imaging of small laboratory animals, mainly mice and rats. Its strength lies in combining several preclinical modalities, so one animal can be studied with magnetic resonance, nuclear and X-ray methods as well as ultrasound and optical imaging. CAPI is a single facility at Charles University Prague, First Faculty of Medicine, in Prague, and it works entirely in biomedical imaging. It has been part of Czech-BioImaging since 2016 and has served Euro-BioImaging users since 2021.

The staff are experienced in multimodal in vivo studies. They register images from different scanners to each other, so a tracer signal can be read against the anatomy around it. The team also develops and tests tracers and imaging probes, and it characterises contrast media and other materials for their imaging behaviour. This expertise supports the development of diagnostic and theranostic procedures. On the magnetic resonance side, staff run protocols for spectroscopy and diffusion imaging. They also run gated cardiac imaging and imaging of nuclei other than hydrogen.

Projects that follow a disease model or a treatment in the same animals make the most of CAPI, because several modalities sit in one place. Groups developing a new radiotracer or contrast agent can test it in living animals and compare its distribution across nuclear imaging and MRI. Materials researchers studying the magnetic or imaging properties of new compounds and particles will also find relevant expertise. Studies that need quick anatomical screening of many animals fit well too, because MRI is offered at both lower and higher field strength.

## Imaging technologies offered

Magnetic resonance is at the centre of what CAPI offers. The 7T MRI (MR Solutions) gives detailed soft-tissue anatomy and supports MR spectroscopy and diffusion tensor imaging. It also handles relaxometry and gated cardiac imaging, so researchers can measure tissue structure and function without sectioning. The Bruker ICON 1T MRI is set up for faster anatomical screening of mice and rats. MRI at both field strengths is also available for ex vivo specimens.

Nuclear imaging shows where a tracer goes and how quickly it gets there. The Bruker Albira CT/PET/SPECT combines micro-PET, micro-SPECT and micro-CT in one instrument, so tracer signals can be placed within the animal's anatomy. CAPI also offers micro-PET/MRI, which sets tracer data against soft-tissue and physiological MR measurements. Micro-CT is available in vivo and ex vivo for three-dimensional anatomy, including bone.

The VEVO LAZR-X (FujiFilm) provides micro-ultrasound and photoacoustic imaging. Ultrasound shows organ structure and blood flow without ionising radiation, while photoacoustic imaging maps blood vessels and light-absorbing agents. In vivo optical imaging follows fluorescent or bioluminescent reporters across the whole animal in repeated sessions. Magnetic particle imaging adds a way to track magnetic nanoparticle tracers against very little background signal.

## Specialties and research focus

Research at CAPI centres on small-animal models, mainly mice and rats, used across biomedical research. A large part of the work concerns new imaging agents. Groups bring radiotracers and other probes for development and testing, and the facility follows how these agents behave in living animals. This work feeds directly into the development of diagnostic and theranostic procedures, which depend on knowing where an agent goes in the body.

Contrast media form a second line of research. CAPI characterises contrast agents and other materials, so materials research sits alongside the biological studies rather than apart from them. The questions here concern how a new compound or particle performs as an imaging agent, and they can be addressed both in the animal and on excised specimens.

These strands often meet in a single project. A probe may be designed and characterised as a material, then tested in a mouse or rat model, and finally judged by how well it supports a diagnostic or theranostic procedure. The facility's application areas cover each of these steps, from biomedical research through tracer development to material research.

## Services and user support

A project at CAPI usually begins with planning, where staff help design the study protocol and set up standard operating procedures. Researchers can then be trained to use the infrastructure, with training workstations and a seminar room on site, or they can work with technical assistance from staff at the instruments.

The facility supports the work around imaging as well. Mice and rats can be bred and housed in individually ventilated cages, and visiting groups can use wet labs and cell culture facilities. Staff develop, test and validate novel imaging probes. A separate radiochemistry and dissection lab offers a cryotome, autoradiography, an automatic gamma counter and radio-HPLC. Analytical and physico-chemical characterisation of probes can be arranged on demand in a partner's chemistry and radiochemistry labs. A 0.5T relaxometer measures the magnetic properties of materials and probes.

Further labs extend the project beyond imaging. A flow cytometry lab offers an imaging flow cytometer with multicolour analysis and sorting, and a molecular biology lab runs real-time PCR. Custom parts can be made with FDM and SLA 3D printers. Once images are acquired, staff help with data processing and analysis, and data can be stored at the facility.

CAPI in Prague is the Node's only facility, and it belongs to the First Faculty of Medicine at Charles University. It brings magnetic resonance, nuclear, X-ray, ultrasound, photoacoustic and optical imaging of small animals together at one site, which suits projects that need several readouts from the same animals. The laboratories around the scanners carry much of the preparatory work, from animal housing and cell culture to radiochemistry and the characterisation of probes. This makes the facility a practical base for studies that run from preparing an imaging agent to testing it in vivo and analysing the results without moving between institutions. Because MRI and micro-CT are also offered ex vivo, the same study can continue on dissected tissue once imaging in the living animal is complete.
