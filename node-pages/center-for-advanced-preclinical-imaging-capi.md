# Center for Advanced Preclinical Imaging (CAPI)

## Overview

The Center for Advanced Preclinical Imaging (CAPI) offers multimodal in vivo imaging of small laboratory animals, mainly mice and rats. Its methods span magnetic resonance, nuclear, X-ray, ultrasound and optical imaging, and the staff register images from these modalities to one another. The Node consists of one facility, CAPI at the First Faculty of Medicine of Charles University in Prague, and it covers biomedical imaging.

CAPI's staff have expertise in three areas. The first is preclinical in vivo imaging itself, including multimodal co-registration, which places a tracer signal on the anatomy of the same animal. The second is the development and testing of tracers and imaging probes, which supports work on new diagnostic and theranostic procedures. The third is the characterisation of contrast media and other materials, so the team supports materials research as well as biomedical studies. CAPI has been part of Czech-BioImaging since 2016.

All of the Node's methods are at CAPI in Prague. Preclinical magnetic resonance imaging and spectroscopy (MRI/MRS) run at fields below 7 tesla and at or above 7 tesla, on living animals and on ex vivo specimens. Nuclear imaging covers positron emission tomography (micro-PET) and single-photon emission computed tomography (micro-SPECT). Both also run as hybrids with micro-CT, and micro-PET is combined with MRI as well. Micro-CT also works on its own for living animals and ex vivo samples. Micro-ultrasound, photoacoustic imaging and in vivo optical imaging add methods that use no ionising radiation.

## Imaging technologies offered

Magnetic resonance is the largest group of methods at CAPI. The 7T MRI (MR Solutions) gives precise anatomical images and MR spectroscopy, along with diffusion-weighted imaging, diffusion tensor imaging and relaxometry. It also runs cardiac imaging gated to the heartbeat, and imaging or spectroscopy of nuclei other than hydrogen. The Bruker ICON 1T MRI handles high-throughput anatomical screening of mice and rats. Both field ranges also image ex vivo specimens.

Nuclear imaging lets researchers follow where a radioactive tracer goes in the body and how its distribution changes over time. The Bruker Albira CT/PET/SPECT combines micro-PET and micro-SPECT with micro-CT, so tracer signals sit on the animal's anatomy. Micro-PET is also combined with MRI, which adds soft-tissue contrast to the tracer data. Micro-CT gives 3D anatomy, including bone and other mineralised structures, in living animals and in ex vivo samples.

The VEVO LAZR-X (FujiFilm) provides micro-ultrasound for quick views of organs and blood flow. The same system performs photoacoustic imaging, which shows vasculature and light-absorbing molecules in tissue. In vivo optical imaging reads fluorescence or bioluminescence reporters in the intact animal, so the same reporter can be measured repeatedly. Magnetic particle imaging adds a further way to track magnetic nanoparticle tracers against very little background from the tissue.

## Specialties and research focus

CAPI's work centres on in vivo studies of mice and rats for biomedical research. The facility supports the development of diagnostic and theranostic procedures, which link the detection of disease with its treatment. Tracer development and testing is a second focus, and the team works on new imaging probes from their characterisation through to testing in animals.

Contrast media form a third focus. CAPI characterises how contrast agents and other materials behave, which takes its work beyond biology into materials research. Alongside living animals, the facility studies ex vivo specimens with MRI and micro-CT. Because the staff co-register images from several modalities, one study at CAPI combines tracer distribution, anatomy and physiology measured in the same mouse or rat.

## Services and user support

A project at CAPI starts with planning, where staff help design the study protocol and set up standard operating procedures. Researchers are then trained in using the infrastructure, with training workstations and a seminar room on site. Staff also give technical assistance with running the instruments. Mice and rats are bred and housed in individually ventilated cages, and wet labs, cell culture facilities and a molecular biology lab with rtPCR support the work around imaging.

For tracer studies, CAPI develops and validates novel imaging probes. A separate radiochemistry and dissection lab has a cryotome, autoradiography and an automatic gamma counter, together with radio-HPLC. Analytical and physico-chemical characterisation of probes takes place on demand in a partner's chemistry and radiochemistry labs. A 0.5T relaxometer measures the magnetic properties of materials and probes. The flow cytometry lab offers imaging flow cytometry, multicolour analysis and sorting, and FDM and SLA printers produce 3D-printed parts. After imaging, staff help with data processing and analysis, and data are stored at the facility.

CAPI is a research facility at the First Faculty of Medicine of Charles University in Prague, and it is the Node's only site. It has provided multimodal preclinical in vivo imaging since 2016 for academic, clinical and industrial partners. Its methods span magnetic resonance, nuclear, X-ray, acoustic and optical imaging, and several of them run in combined hybrid systems.

The team's expertise lies in imaging small laboratory animals and in co-registering images across modalities. Staff also develop and test tracers and imaging probes, and they characterise contrast media and other materials. This work links the facility to diagnostics and theranostics development and to materials research, alongside its core biomedical studies. The lower-field MRI shares its location with PET, SPECT, CT, magnetic particle imaging and optical imaging.
