# Danish BioImaging Node (DBI)

## Overview

The Danish BioImaging Node (DBI) is built around advanced light microscopy, which five of its six facilities offer. Around that core it adds super-resolution and functional microscopy, volume electron microscopy at CFIB in Copenhagen and preclinical imaging at the Aarhus University Bioimaging Core Facility in Aarhus. Four facilities belong to the University of Copenhagen, and one of these is at its Biotech Research and Innovation Centre (BRIC). The other two are part of Aarhus University and the University of Southern Denmark in Odense, so the Node spans four cities and covers both biological and biomedical imaging.

The staff's expertise differs from site to site. CAB in Frederiksberg specialises in functional microscopy and histology and runs an experimental microscopy lab. DaMBIC in Odense supports light microscopy of specimens from single molecules to cells and tissue. At LASM in Copenhagen, the team combines laboratory automation with high-content screening. The image analysts at IACF in Copenhagen develop workflows for individual projects, including machine learning, deep learning and 3D registration. In Aarhus, the core facility pairs microscopy with image analysis and microscopy teaching. On the preclinical side, Node staff have experience imaging whole-animal metabolism and cardiovascular function, including in pigs and exotic animal models.

Cell biologists who need nanoscale detail or a three-dimensional view of cell ultrastructure will find the most options at CFIB, which offers both super-resolution and volume electron microscopy. Groups running plate-based screens of cells or libraries should look first to LASM. Projects that follow anatomy, physiology or metabolism in living animals belong at the Aarhus University Bioimaging Core Facility, which offers preclinical MRI, PET/MRI and ultrasound.

## Imaging technologies offered

Confocal microscopy is the backbone of the Node and is available at all five microscopy facilities. All five also offer spinning disk systems for fast optical sectioning with less light exposure, which suits live cells. Image scanning microscopy at the Aarhus University Bioimaging Core Facility, CFIB and LASM makes better use of the collected light to give sharper images of cells and subcellular structures.

For structures below the diffraction limit, STED is offered at CFIB and DaMBIC, and single-molecule localisation microscopy at CFIB, CAB and DaMBIC. CFIB and CAB also offer FLIM, FRET and FRAP, which measure molecular environment, proximity and mobility inside cells. Label-free chemical imaging by Raman spectroscopy is available at CAB and DaMBIC, and CAB adds harmonic generation imaging of ordered tissue structures. Light-sheet microscopy at the Aarhus University Bioimaging Core Facility and CFIB images embryos and organs, and both facilities offer intravital microscopy of cells in living animals. High-content screening is offered at four facilities.

Electron microscopy centres on CFIB, which offers FIB-SEM and serial block-face SEM to reconstruct cells and tissue in three dimensions. Scanning electron microscopy is available at CFIB and DaMBIC. Representative systems include the STEDYCON at CFIB, the Lightsheet 7 in Aarhus and the ImageXpress Confocal HT.ai at LASM.

Preclinical imaging is concentrated at the Aarhus University Bioimaging Core Facility, with MRI at 7 T or above and PET/MRI for anatomy, physiology and tracer studies. The facility also offers micro-CT and ultrasound, while LASM provides in vivo optical imaging.

## Specialties and research focus

The research these facilities serve runs from molecules to whole organisms. The Node's work spans plant biology, zoology and pathology, along with high-content screening of yeast libraries. LASM concentrates on cell-based screening that is not tied to any one disease, while IACF supports analysis across life-science and medical imaging. DaMBIC works with specimens from single molecules to cells and tissue, through one unit serving the natural sciences and one serving the health sciences. CAB brings histology expertise to its microscopy, which suits studies of tissue organisation.

In biomedical research the Node turns to living animals. Its preclinical work covers in vivo and ex vivo imaging of exotic animal models, and it holds a large repository of animal imaging data covering more than 5,000 species. Metabolic studies follow whole-animal metabolism as well as the oxygen metabolism of the heart and kidneys. Cardiovascular research looks at blood flow and heart function in living animals.

## Services and user support

Work with the Node can begin with training in project planning and management and in methodological setup, so that the experiment is designed around the right method. Visitors then receive an introduction to the facility and training in image acquisition, and staff can give technical assistance to run instruments. General training courses are offered as well.

For sample work, the Node provides wet lab and chemistry lab space, cell culture facilities and animal facilities, together with model organisms and a high biological safety level. Probes are available, including a repository of molecular probes, and a radiochemistry lab supports tracer studies. Biomedical projects can draw on biobanking and patient subject recruitment. After acquisition, training in image processing and analysis helps researchers turn images into results, and workstations, desks and ICT access are available to visitors. Images can be kept in the Node's data storage, and the facilities work with a high level of infrastructure integration.

The Node has six facilities, and four of them are in the Copenhagen area. CFIB is the broadest, bringing light and electron microscopy together in one place. A project can therefore move from fluorescence imaging to ultrastructure without changing site. CAB complements it with functional and label-free microscopy and with photomanipulation, which lets researchers perturb selected cells or molecules with light and watch the response.

LASM at BRIC is a screening and automation core, and it suits projects that need many conditions imaged and measured in the same way. IACF is an analysis facility rather than an imaging one. Its image analysts advise researchers and build analysis workflows with them, in person or remotely, and both CFIB and the Aarhus University Bioimaging Core Facility link their users to it.

Outside Copenhagen, DaMBIC in Odense runs two units, one serving the natural sciences and one serving the health sciences. Its strength is light microscopy, including STED and Raman-based imaging, and it also offers scanning electron microscopy. The Aarhus University Bioimaging Core Facility is the Node's site for preclinical MRI and PET/MRI. It also runs light-sheet, two-photon and intravital microscopy, so one project there can combine whole-animal imaging with cellular detail.
