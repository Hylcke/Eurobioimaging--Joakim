# Danish BioImaging Node (DBI)

## Overview

The Danish BioImaging Node (DBI) mainly offers advanced light microscopy, from confocal and light-sheet imaging to super-resolution and functional fluorescence methods. It also offers electron microscopy, automated high-content screening and image analysis. The Node has six facilities at four institutions in Aarhus, Copenhagen, Frederiksberg and Odense, and it covers both biological and biomedical imaging, although most of its methods look at cells and tissues.

Each facility brings its own expertise. The Aarhus University Bioimaging Core Facility in Aarhus has long experience in microscopy education and image data analysis. CAB in Frederiksberg combines light and electron microscopy with functional microscopy and histology, and it runs an Experimental Microscopy Lab. DaMBIC in Odense covers light microscopy of specimens from single molecules to cells and tissue, with one site in natural sciences and one in health sciences at the University of Southern Denmark. Staff at LASM in Copenhagen are expert in laboratory automation and cell-based screening. The image analysts and programmers at IACF in Copenhagen develop analysis workflows for individual projects, including machine learning, deep learning and 3D registration.

Laser scanning and spinning disk confocal microscopy are at all five microscopy facilities. CFIB in Copenhagen offers volume electron microscopy by FIB-SEM and serial block-face SEM, and scanning electron microscopy is at CFIB and DaMBIC. STED is at CFIB and DaMBIC, and single-molecule localisation microscopy is at CFIB, CAB and DaMBIC. Functional fluorescence methods are at CFIB and CAB, while Raman-based label-free imaging is at CAB and DaMBIC. Light-sheet and intravital microscopy are at CFIB and the Aarhus University Bioimaging Core Facility, in vivo optical imaging is at LASM, and image analysis is at IACF and CFIB.

## Imaging technologies offered

Confocal microscopy is the backbone of the Node. Laser scanning and spinning disk systems give optical sections and multichannel 3D localisation in cells and tissues, and image scanning microscopy draws finer detail from the same signal. Two-photon microscopy at four facilities reaches cells deep inside thick tissue. For structures below the diffraction limit, STED and single-molecule localisation microscopy map how proteins are arranged within subcellular structures, and CFIB runs a STEDYCON for STED. Fluorescence lifetime, energy transfer and photobleaching assays at CFIB and CAB measure molecular proximity, mobility and exchange inside cells. Label-free methods at CAB and DaMBIC, including Raman spectroscopy, read the chemical composition of cells and tissue without a dye, and CAB adds harmonic generation imaging of ordered tissue structures.

For larger specimens, the Lightsheet 7 at the Aarhus University Bioimaging Core Facility images embryos and organs plane by plane with little excess light. Automated screening at four facilities compares cell phenotypes across many wells and conditions. LASM runs plate-based screens on an ImageXpress Confocal HT.ai and follows cells over time on an IncuCyte S3. Electron microscopy adds the ultrastructure. FIB-SEM and serial block-face SEM at CFIB reconstruct cells and tissue in 3D at electron resolution, and scanning electron microscopy at CFIB and DaMBIC shows surface morphology. LASM also offers in vivo optical imaging of fluorescent and bioluminescent reporters in intact animals.

## Specialties and research focus

The Node's research spans cell biology, plant biology, zoology and pathology. High-content screening of yeast libraries is one of the lines of work it supports.

At LASM in Copenhagen the focus is cell-based screening that is not tied to any one disease, so the same automated pipelines serve many kinds of biological question. CAB in Frederiksberg links microscopy with histology, which brings tissue sections into its work alongside cells. DaMBIC in Odense serves both the natural sciences and the health sciences through its two sites, and its work runs from single molecules to cells and tissue. The Aarhus University Bioimaging Core Facility belongs to the Department of Biomedicine and includes imaging in living animals through intravital microscopy. IACF in Copenhagen serves life-science and medical bioimaging across Denmark, so the datasets it works with come from microscopy as well as from medical imaging.

## Services and user support

A project can begin with training in project planning and management and in methodological setup, so that the method and the experimental design fit the question. Researchers then receive an introduction to the facility, technical assistance in running the instruments and training in image acquisition, and the Node also runs general training courses. Its infrastructure is closely integrated across the facilities.

For the experimental work, the Node provides wet lab and cell culture facilities, a chemistry lab and a radiochemistry lab, and work at a high biological safety level. It also provides animal facilities and model organisms, together with probes and a repository of molecular probes. Biomedical projects can draw on biobanking and patient subject recruitment. Once images are acquired, staff train researchers in image processing and analysis. Visitors have workstations, desks and ICT access during their stay, and the Node stores image data for its projects.

Two facilities centre on light microscopy. The Aarhus University Bioimaging Core Facility offers confocal, light-sheet and intravital microscopy, and its staff teach microscopy and image data analysis. DaMBIC covers light microscopy from single molecules to tissue, including STED and Raman-based imaging, and it also offers scanning electron microscopy.

CFIB and CAB, both at the University of Copenhagen, bring light and electron microscopy together. CFIB offers volume electron microscopy and cryo-TEM next to super-resolution and functional fluorescence methods, and it is one of two facilities that offer image analysis. CAB has expertise in functional microscopy and histology, and its Experimental Microscopy Lab supports advanced light microscopy. CAB offers label-free methods such as harmonic generation imaging, and it is the only facility in the Node that offers photomanipulation, which lets researchers perturb or label chosen targets with light.

LASM, at the Biotech Research and Innovation Centre, is a screening and automation facility. It combines assay automation with high-content imaging, deconvolution widefield microscopy and in vivo optical imaging for cell-based screens. IACF is a dedicated image analysis facility without imaging instruments of its own. Its analysts and programmers handle both biological and medical image analysis and build workflows for quantifying 3D and time-lapse datasets.
