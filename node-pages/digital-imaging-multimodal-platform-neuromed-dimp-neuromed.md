# Digital Imaging Multimodal Platform Neuromed (DIMP)

## Overview

The Digital Imaging Multimodal Platform Neuromed (DIMP) mainly offers preclinical imaging of animal models for neuroscience research. It also supports clinical neuroscience studies, and its work spans animal-model research through to clinical translation. The Node has one facility, the Digital Imaging Multimodal Platform Neuromed at the Neuromed Mediterranean Neurological Institute (IRCCS) in Pozzilli, and it covers both biological and biomedical imaging.

Staff at the platform are experienced in neuroscience studies that move between animal models and human subjects. They run mouse-clinic studies, in which a mouse model is characterised with several imaging methods rather than just one. Their expertise also covers cardiocirculatory phenotyping, which measures how the heart and circulation work in animal models. On the clinical side, the team supports population imaging studies, from setting up a cohort to preparing and processing the imaging data. Image analysis is a core part of their work, and staff handle both preclinical and clinical image data with tailored software where a study needs it.

All of the Node's methods are at the Pozzilli facility, which combines three main groups. Its preclinical imaging covers anatomy and function with high-field MRI and MR spectroscopy, micro-CT and micro-ultrasound. Molecular and reporter signals in living animals are followed with micro-PET/CT and in vivo optical imaging. Human imaging takes the form of advanced support for population imaging studies, and medical image analysis covers data from both animal and human studies.

## Imaging technologies offered

Preclinical imaging is the largest part of the Node. High-field MRI at 7 tesla or above shows organs and tissues inside living animals without any sectioning, and MR spectroscopy adds measurements of tissue chemistry. Micro-CT builds three-dimensional images of anatomy, including bone and other mineralised structures. Micro-ultrasound gives quick views of organ structure and blood flow in accessible vessels, and it uses no ionising radiation. For molecular questions, micro-PET/CT follows the distribution of a radioactive tracer and places it on a CT image of the animal's anatomy. In vivo optical imaging reads fluorescent or bioluminescent reporters in intact animals, which lets researchers measure the same biological signal again over the course of a study. Its signals come from whole animals or organs rather than single cells.

The clinical and data methods support the step towards patients. Advanced support for population imaging studies helps researchers design cohort studies and prepare, process and analyse the resulting imaging data. Medical image analysis turns acquired images into quantitative and reproducible results.

The platform's preclinical MRI system runs at 7T. Its clinical 3T MRI scanner has a 64-channel brain coil and a cardiac coil, which match the Node's work on the brain and the circulation.

## Specialties and research focus

The facility's research focus is neuroscience, approached as a translational path from animal models to clinical studies. Its preclinical work centres on mouse models, which the platform studies in the manner of a mouse clinic. Each model is examined with several complementary readouts, so that anatomy, function and molecular signals can be compared within the same study.

Cardiocirculatory phenotyping is the second research area the platform serves. These studies look at how the heart and blood vessels behave in mouse models, and they sit alongside the neuroscience work at the same site.

On the human side, the facility works with population imaging, meaning imaging studies of groups of people rather than individual cases. Here the questions concern patterns across a cohort, and the work depends on careful study design and consistent processing of large sets of images. Clinical and preclinical image analysis connects these strands, because the same team works with data from mouse studies and from human cohorts within one neuroscience institute.

The Digital Imaging Multimodal Platform Neuromed in Pozzilli is the Node's only facility, and it belongs to the Neuromed Mediterranean Neurological Institute (IRCCS). It brings preclinical multimodal molecular imaging and clinical population imaging together on one site, with digital image analysis linking the two sides. The preclinical side combines five imaging methods, from high-field MRI and MR spectroscopy to in vivo optical imaging, for studies in mouse models of neurological conditions and cardiocirculatory function. The clinical side supports population imaging studies of human cohorts, covering study design, data preparation and processing, and staff analyse medical images from both animal and human work. The team's expertise lies in translational neuroscience, mouse-clinic studies that characterise a model with several methods, and cardiocirculatory phenotyping in mice.
