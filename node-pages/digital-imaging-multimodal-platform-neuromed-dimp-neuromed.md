# Digital Imaging Multimodal Platform Neuromed (DIMP)

## Overview

The Digital Imaging Multimodal Platform Neuromed (DIMP) is built around preclinical imaging of mouse models for neuroscience. Its preclinical methods cover the main whole-animal modalities, and its support for population imaging studies carries the same questions into human cohorts. The Node is a single facility, the Digital Imaging Multimodal Platform Neuromed in Pozzilli, run by the Neuromed Mediterranean Neurological Institute (IRCCS). It covers both biological and biomedical imaging, from two-photon microscopy of tissue to clinical imaging data, and it has dedicated computing for analysis.

The staff work mainly in neuroscience and have experience running mouse-clinic studies, in which one animal model is characterised with several imaging methods. They support both preclinical and clinical image analysis, so the same team can help quantify data from animal scans and from patient or cohort imaging. Population imaging is a second area of expertise. Here the staff help with study design, secure data preparation and the processing of imaging data collected from groups of people. Cardiocirculatory phenotyping is also among the research areas the platform supports, alongside its neuroscience work.

The Node suits translational neuroscience projects that start in a mouse model and aim to connect their findings with human imaging. Groups that follow disease progression or treatment in the same animals can combine magnetic resonance, CT, ultrasound, PET and optical imaging at one site. Researchers running cohort or clinical imaging studies will find support for setting up the study and handling its data. Groups that phenotype the heart and circulation in mouse models can also use the preclinical side of the platform.

## Imaging technologies offered

Preclinical imaging is the Node's largest family. Micro-MRI and magnetic resonance spectroscopy at 7 tesla or above show soft-tissue anatomy and physiology in rodents, and spectroscopy adds a measure of tissue chemistry. Because none of these methods needs sectioning, the same animal can be scanned repeatedly over a study. Micro-CT gives 3D anatomy, including bone and other mineralised structures. Micro-ultrasound (micro-US) gives fast views of organs and blood flow without ionising radiation. Micro-PET/CT follows the distribution of a radioactive tracer by positron emission tomography and places it on a CT image of the anatomy. In vivo optical imaging records fluorescent or bioluminescent reporters across the whole animal, which suits repeated reporter measurements in the same model.

Light microscopy is represented by two-photon microscopy, which images cells deep within thick tissue. On the human side, the Node supports population imaging studies with help on cohort set-up, secure data preparation and storage, processing and tailored software. Medical image analysis supports quantification and reproducible processing of both preclinical and clinical data. The instruments include a 7T preclinical MRI scanner, a micro-CT and a micro-ultrasound system, as well as a 3T clinical MRI scanner with a 64-channel brain coil and a cardiac coil.

## Specialties and research focus

Research at the Node centres on neuroscience, with a translational aim of linking findings in animal models to clinical studies. The preclinical side is organised as a mouse clinic, in which each mouse model is characterised with several imaging modalities rather than one. This approach fits questions about how a neurological condition develops in a model and how it responds to an intervention over time.

Cardiocirculatory phenotyping is a second application area. It concerns the heart and circulation in mouse models, and so extends the platform's work beyond the nervous system. Clinical and population imaging form the other half of the research focus. The facility supports cohort studies in which imaging data from many people are collected, prepared and analysed together. Its expertise in clinical and preclinical analysis means that measurements from animals and from people can be handled by the same team. A research question can therefore move from a mouse model to patient or cohort data within one institute in Pozzilli.

## Services and user support

A project usually starts with methodological set-up, where staff help choose the imaging methods and plan the protocol. While the study runs, they give technical assistance and support the acquisition. For preclinical work, the animal facilities include the mouse clinic and breeding, so mouse colonies can be kept at the same site where they are imaged.

Wet lab space and cell culture facilities are part of the platform, which lets sample work take place alongside imaging. A chemistry lab and a radiochemistry lab support studies that need chemical or radioactive preparations, which is relevant to tracer studies with micro-PET/CT. Biobanking allows samples from a study to be kept for later analysis. Once data are acquired, staff help with processing and analysis, and the Node offers data storage for the results.

The Digital Imaging Multimodal Platform Neuromed in Pozzilli is the Node's only facility, and it belongs to the Neuromed Mediterranean Neurological Institute (IRCCS). It combines a preclinical arm for multimodal molecular imaging with a clinical arm for population imaging, and dedicated computing links the two. Researchers can run their own studies at the platform or work with staff on design and analysis. Because animal housing, laboratories and imaging sit at one site, a mouse study can move from breeding to scanning and analysis without moving between institutions. The clinical arm suits groups whose project needs human imaging data alongside animal work, as well as groups running a cohort study on its own. Together the two arms let a neuroscience question be tested in a mouse model and then followed into human imaging data at the same institute.
