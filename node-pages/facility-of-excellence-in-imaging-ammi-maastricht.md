# Facility of Multimodal Imaging - AMMI Maastricht

## Overview

The Advanced Microscopy and Molecular Imaging (AMMI) Node in Maastricht combines advanced light microscopy, mass spectrometry imaging and preclinical imaging. It links imaging at three scales, from the molecular composition of tissue sections and the behaviour of cells in tissue to whole animals imaged non-invasively. Its three facilities are all at Maastricht University in Maastricht, and together they cover both biological and biomedical imaging.

Staff at the Mass Spectrometry Imaging CORE Laboratory map the spatial distribution of intact biomolecules in tissue at resolutions from 200 nm to 200 µm. Their expertise spans lipids, metabolites, proteins and glycans, and they work on spatial biology and the analysis of complex surfaces. At the Microscopy Core Lab, staff combine advanced light microscopy with in situ molecular detection, sample preparation and software support. The same team handles data processing and image analysis for the projects it supports. The Preclinical Imaging team is experienced in in vivo molecular imaging and works within the CARIM and M4I research environment of Maastricht University.

Confocal, two-photon and STED microscopy are at the Microscopy Core Lab in Maastricht, which also offers fluorescence lifetime imaging and intravital microscopy. Mass spectrometry imaging of biological and medical samples is at the Mass Spectrometry Imaging CORE Laboratory in Maastricht. Micro-CT, micro-PET and micro-SPECT are at Preclinical Imaging in Maastricht.

## Imaging technologies offered

Preclinical Imaging offers micro-CT, micro-PET and micro-SPECT for studies in small animals. Micro-CT shows 3D anatomy, including bone and other mineralised structures, without the need for physical sectioning. Micro-PET and micro-SPECT measure where a radioactive tracer goes in the body and how its distribution changes over time, so researchers can follow molecular processes in living animals.

The Mass Spectrometry Imaging CORE Laboratory adds chemical information to the picture. Mass spectrometry imaging measures which lipids, metabolites and peptides are present at each point of a tissue section, and so shows how these molecules are distributed across the tissue. Its instruments include a 9.4T MALDI/ESI SolariX FT-ICR imaging mass spectrometer, a Thermo Fisher Orbitrap Elite and a Bruker Ultraflex III ToF/ToF MALDI imager with IonPix camera.

Light microscopy at the Microscopy Core Lab covers confocal, two-photon and STED microscopy. Laser scanning confocal microscopy gives optical sections and multichannel 3D localisation in cells and tissue. Two-photon microscopy confines excitation to the focal plane, which makes it possible to image cells deeper in thick tissue. STED resolves subcellular structures and molecular distributions below the diffraction limit. Fluorescence lifetime imaging (FLIM) adds contrast that reports on the molecular environment and on interactions between molecules. Intravital microscopy (IVM) follows cellular processes inside living animals, in their intact physiological context.

## Specialties and research focus

Through Maastricht University's research schools, the Node collaborates with groups working in cardiovascular, oncological, orthopaedic, metabolic, neurological and regenerative medicine. These fields set many of the questions the three facilities work on, and they explain the Node's mix of tissue-level molecular imaging and whole-animal imaging.

The Mass Spectrometry Imaging CORE Laboratory works with biological tissues and prepared molecular-imaging specimens. Its applications include spatial biology, biomedical tissue analysis and clinical translation, together with the analysis of complex surfaces. The laboratory also carries out research on intraoperative tissue classification, which uses molecular signatures to tell tissue types apart during surgery.

The Microscopy Core Lab serves projects in the life sciences and medicine, and it also supports work in the materials sciences. Preclinical Imaging focuses on in vivo molecular imaging and is part of the CARIM and M4I research environment at Maastricht University, where it supports studies that follow molecular processes in living animal models.

## Services and user support

Work with the Node usually begins with methodological setup. Staff help design the study protocol and write the standard operating procedures that the project will follow. For animal studies, the Node also covers animal breeding, animal ethical licences, experiment protocols and animal handling, so these steps are planned together with the imaging itself.

Researchers are trained in the use of the infrastructure, with training workstations and a training seminar room for this purpose. During acquisition, staff give technical assistance to run the instruments. The Node also prepares probes for imaging experiments, and wet lab space is available to visiting researchers for their own sample work.

Once data are acquired, the Node offers data storage and analysis. This includes artificial intelligence and deep learning approaches, and radiomics, which extracts quantitative features from medical and preclinical images for further analysis.

The Microscopy Core Lab is the Node's light microscopy platform. It offers confocal, two-photon and STED microscopy alongside fluorescence lifetime imaging and intravital microscopy. Its staff bring together microscopy, in situ molecular detection, sample preparation and software support, and they handle data processing and image analysis. The lab serves the life sciences, medicine and materials sciences.

The Mass Spectrometry Imaging CORE Laboratory specialises in spatially resolved molecular imaging of tissues and complex surfaces. It offers mass spectrometry imaging for both biological and medical samples, mapping intact biomolecules at resolutions from 200 nm to 200 µm. Its staff are expert in the analysis of lipids, metabolites, proteins and glycans, and they work on spatial biology, biomedical tissue analysis and clinical translation. The laboratory also researches intraoperative tissue classification.

Preclinical Imaging provides the Node's non-invasive whole-animal methods, which are micro-CT, micro-PET and micro-SPECT. Its expertise lies in preclinical and in vivo molecular imaging, and it works within the CARIM and M4I research environment at Maastricht University. The three facilities share one institution and one city, so a project can draw on the light microscopy, molecular imaging and preclinical teams at the same university.
