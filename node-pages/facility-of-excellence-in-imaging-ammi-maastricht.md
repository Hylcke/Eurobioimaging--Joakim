# Facility of Multimodal Imaging - AMMI Maastricht

## Overview

The Advanced Microscopy and Molecular Imaging (AMMI) Node in Maastricht brings together imaging at very different scales, from molecules in a tissue section to whole living animals. Its strength lies in combining advanced light microscopy, mass spectrometry imaging and preclinical in vivo imaging within one university. A biomedical question can therefore be followed from the organism down to the cell and the molecule. The Node has three facilities, all at Maastricht University in Maastricht, and it covers both biological and biomedical imaging.

Staff at the Microscopy Core Lab work with fluorescence imaging from subcellular structures to living animals, including super-resolution and lifetime-based measurements. They also support data processing and image analysis. The team at the Mass Spectrometry Imaging CORE Laboratory specialises in spatially resolved molecular imaging of biological tissues and complex surfaces. They map intact biomolecules at resolutions between 200 nanometres and 200 micrometres, and their experience spans proteomics, lipidomics, metabolomics and glycomics. Preclinical Imaging brings expertise in preclinical and in vivo molecular imaging, and it works within the CARIM and M4I research environment at Maastricht University.

Cell biologists who need confocal, two-photon or super-resolution imaging will find most support at the Microscopy Core Lab. Researchers in spatial biology or clinical tissue analysis who want to see where lipids, metabolites or peptides sit within a tissue section should turn to the Mass Spectrometry Imaging CORE Laboratory. Groups that follow tracers, anatomy or disease in small animals will work mainly with Preclinical Imaging. Projects that need more than one of these views can combine them without leaving the Node.

## Imaging technologies offered

Light microscopy at the Node is concentrated in the Microscopy Core Lab. Laser scanning confocal microscopy gives optical sections and multichannel 3D localisation in cells and thin tissues. Two-photon microscopy confines excitation to the focal plane, so cells can be imaged deeper within thick tissue. Intravital microscopy takes this further and lets researchers watch cellular processes inside a living animal, in their physiological context. For finer detail, stimulated emission depletion (STED) microscopy resolves subcellular structures well below the diffraction limit. Fluorescence lifetime imaging (FLIM) adds contrast that reports on the molecular environment and on interactions between molecules, beyond what intensity alone shows.

Preclinical Imaging offers micro-PET and micro-SPECT, which measure where a radioactive tracer goes in the body and how its distribution changes over time. Micro-CT at the same facility shows 3D anatomy, including bone and other mineralised structures, without cutting the animal into sections.

The Node also offers mass spectrometry-based imaging at the Mass Spectrometry Imaging CORE Laboratory. This maps the spatial distribution of lipids, metabolites and peptides across a tissue section without labels. Instruments include a 9.4T MALDI/ESI SolariX FT-ICR imaging mass spectrometer, a Thermo Fisher Orbitrap Elite and a Bruker Ultraflex III ToF/ToF MALDI imager with an IonPix camera.

## Specialties and research focus

Research at AMMI Maastricht grows out of collaborations with the university's research schools. These span cardiovascular, oncological, orthopaedic, metabolic, neurological and regenerative medicine, so much of the work served by the Node has a clear biomedical direction.

The Mass Spectrometry Imaging CORE Laboratory works in spatial biology and biomedical tissue analysis. Its samples are biological tissues and specimens prepared for molecular imaging, and the questions usually concern which molecules are present in a tissue and where they are found. The laboratory also works on clinical translation, bringing molecular imaging closer to questions that arise in patient care. Complex surface analysis forms a further part of its research.

The Microscopy Core Lab serves projects in the life sciences and medicine, and it also supports work in the materials sciences. Preclinical Imaging focuses on in vivo molecular imaging within the CARIM and M4I research environment. Together, the three facilities support research that moves between tissue samples, cells and living animal models within the medical fields of the research schools.

## Services and user support

A project at AMMI Maastricht usually starts with methodological setup. Staff help design the study protocol and write the standard operating procedures, so the experiment is planned around the right method from the start. Researchers can then be trained in using the infrastructure, with training workstations and a seminar room available for teaching. During acquisition, staff give technical assistance to run the instruments.

The Node also prepares probes for imaging experiments. For animal studies it covers animal breeding, animal ethical licences, animal experiment protocols and animal handling, which means visiting groups do not have to arrange these steps themselves. Wet lab space is available for preparing samples on site.

Once data have been acquired, the Node supports data storage and analysis. Its analysis support extends to artificial intelligence, deep learning and radiomics, which extract quantitative features from images for further study.

All three facilities belong to Maastricht University and sit in the same city, which makes it practical to move a project from one facility to another. Each covers a different scale, and together they link molecular, cellular and whole-animal views of the same biological question.

The Microscopy Core Lab in Maastricht is a research platform for advanced light microscopy, and it also provides sample preparation and software support. It suits projects that need to see cells and subcellular structures, whether in culture, in thick tissue or in a living animal.

The Mass Spectrometry Imaging CORE Laboratory in Maastricht provides service, training and support in mass spectrometry imaging and proteomics. It suits projects that need chemical information with spatial context, such as mapping lipids or metabolites across a diseased tissue. Its work complements fluorescence imaging, because it detects molecules without the need for labels.

Preclinical Imaging in Maastricht is the Node's facility for in vivo imaging of small animals with nuclear imaging and CT. It suits studies of tracer distribution and anatomy in whole animals. A tissue taken from such a study can then be examined at the Mass Spectrometry Imaging CORE Laboratory or the Microscopy Core Lab, so the same model can be studied from organ to molecule.
