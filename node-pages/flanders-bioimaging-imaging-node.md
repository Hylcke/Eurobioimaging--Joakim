# Flanders BioImaging Node (FlaBI)

## Overview

The Flanders BioImaging Node (FlaBI) covers imaging from molecules to patients, with its greatest depth in advanced light microscopy and preclinical imaging of small animals. Six of its facilities offer light microscopy and five offer in vivo imaging, so a project can move from cells to animal models within one Node. FlaBI brings together nine facilities run by seven universities and research institutes. Most sites are in Leuven, Ghent and Antwerp, with further facilities in Brussels and Diepenbeek. The Node spans biological and biomedical imaging. Volume electron microscopy is centred on the VIB BioImaging Core in Ghent and Leuven, and clinical imaging on MIRaCLe in Leuven.

The staff bring experience at every scale the Node covers. CIC in Leuven specialises in live imaging of cells, organs and whole organisms with multiphoton and super-resolution microscopy. AOMC in Diepenbeek focuses on dynamic imaging and light-sheet microscopy, while GLiM in Ghent has particular expertise in fluorescence lifetime imaging. ACAM in Antwerp works on quantitative systems microscopy, with expertise in automation and in imaging whole specimens. Teams at ICMI in Brussels and MICA-BIL Core Facility in Antwerp are experienced in nuclear and optical small-animal imaging. MIRaCLe combines radiopharmacy, nuclear medicine and image analysis, and develops radiotracers through to preclinical and clinical validation. The VIB BioImaging Core is expert in correlative light and electron microscopy.

Cell biologists who need live imaging or nanoscale resolution will find the most options at CIC. Projects that need ultrastructure in three dimensions, or need to place a fluorescent signal in its electron microscopy context, suit the VIB BioImaging Core. Translational studies in oncology or neurodegeneration that follow a tracer from animal models into people are best served by MIRaCLe. Small-animal imaging is also offered in Antwerp, Brussels and Ghent.

## Imaging technologies offered

Light microscopy is the Node's largest family. Laser scanning confocal microscopy is offered at four facilities and two-photon microscopy at five, so researchers can section thick tissue and image cells deep inside living samples. For faster and gentler live imaging, CIC and GLiM run spinning disk confocal systems. CIC adds lattice light-sheet microscopy for following 3D dynamics in cells and embryos. Light-sheet imaging of larger specimens such as organs is offered at AOMC, ACAM and CIC. Below the diffraction limit, CIC offers STED on an Abberior STED Expert line and AOMC offers single-molecule localisation microscopy.

Functional methods measure what molecules do rather than only where they sit. FLIM, FRET and FRAP report molecular environment, proximity and mobility. All three are offered at AOMC, GLiM and the VIB BioImaging Core, and AOMC uses a PicoQuant Luminosa for lifetime work.

Preclinical imaging combines nuclear, magnetic resonance and optical methods in living animals. Micro-PET and micro-SPECT are each offered at three facilities and follow how a tracer distributes and clears over time. MICA-BIL Core Facility runs a Bruker 9.4T MRI with cryo-coil for high-field anatomy and physiology. MIRaCLe offers simultaneous PET/MRI in animals and in people, which pairs tracer biology with soft-tissue detail. In electron microscopy, the VIB BioImaging Core provides volume imaging by FIB-SEM and serial block-face SEM, while AOMC and ACAM offer scanning electron microscopy.

## Specialties and research focus

The Node's research focus is disease, studied from molecules and cells up to organs, whole organisms and patients. Oncology and neurodegenerative disease receive particular attention, and the facilities help develop and validate biomarker tools for drug development and precision medicine. MIRaCLe works across oncology, neurology, cardiology and infection and inflammation, including tracers for tau and amyloid in Alzheimer's disease. MICA-BIL Core Facility concentrates on neuroscience and oncology, and it also supports studies of medical devices and materials. ICMI studies how pharmaceuticals and radiopharmaceuticals behave in the body, covering biodistribution, pharmacokinetics, dosimetry and therapeutic efficacy.

At the cellular scale, CIC serves cell and tissue biology and neurophysiology. It has a particular interest in the enteric nervous system and neurogastroenterology, and its work covers organs, tissue slices and organoids. The VIB BioImaging Core is embedded in VIB research centres for inflammation and for brain and disease research. GLiM supports research across the life sciences.

## Services and user support

Projects often begin with methodological set-up, where staff help design the study protocol and standard operating procedures. For studies with animals or people, the Node supports applications for preclinical and clinical ethical permission and helps recruit patients and subjects for clinical studies. Researchers can then work with technical assistance on the instruments or be trained to use them, and training workstations and a seminar room support this teaching.

Sample work is supported by probe preparation, animal preparation, wet lab space and cell culture facilities at safety levels 1 and 2. Animal facilities include housing, a BSL2 animal facility and a specific-pathogen-free facility for high-field MRI. Researchers can also draw on established disease models, and large-animal scanning is offered for mini-pigs. Tissue taken after an in vivo study can be examined by autoradiography with many isotopes. On the analysis side, the Node offers data processing and analysis and full preclinical and clinical quantitation of PET studies. Server space and data storage come with support for defining and implementing metadata.

FlaBI's nine facilities fall into two groups that complement each other. Five centre on microscopy and four on imaging living animals and people. CIC and ICMI both offer intravital microscopy, which links the two groups by showing cells at work inside a living animal.

Among the microscopy cores, AOMC is a fluorescence microscopy facility with a strong functional side, offering correlation spectroscopy and polarisation microscopy. ACAM focuses on quantitative systems microscopy at meso-, micro- and nanometre scales, and offers high-content screening for experiments across many conditions. GLiM supports life-science research with standard and specialised microscopes, including spinning disk confocal and photomanipulation. The VIB BioImaging Core works from its two sites and combines electron microscopy with light microscopy methods such as FLIM and high-content screening, so one team can follow a sample from fluorescence to ultrastructure.

On the in vivo side, Core ARTH Infinity in Ghent offers non-invasive small-animal imaging that includes micro-MRI, micro-CT and ultrasound. ICMI combines nuclear and optical small-animal imaging with a focus on drug behaviour. MICA-BIL Core Facility joins the expertise of two Antwerp imaging groups in MRI, molecular imaging and CT. MIRaCLe is the Node's only clinical facility, and its preclinical scanners sit in the same centre, so tracers can move from animal studies to patients without changing teams.
