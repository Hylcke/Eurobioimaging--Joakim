# Flanders BioImaging Node (FlaBI)

## Overview

Flanders BioImaging (FlaBI) is a Belgian Node that offers imaging from molecules to patients. Its largest areas are advanced light microscopy and preclinical imaging of small animals, and it adds volume electron microscopy and clinical molecular imaging with PET. The Node has nine facilities at seven institutions in Antwerp, Brussels, Diepenbeek, Ghent and Leuven, and it covers both biological and biomedical imaging.

The Node supports research on disease at the molecular, cellular, organ and whole-organism level, with a particular focus on oncology and neurodegenerative disease. Its staff also help develop and validate imaging biomarkers for drug development and precision medicine. The team at CIC in Leuven specialises in live imaging of cells, organs and whole organisms with multiphoton and super-resolution microscopy. AOMC in Diepenbeek brings expertise in dynamic imaging and light-sheet microscopy, and ACAM in Antwerp works on quantitative systems microscopy and automated imaging. The VIB BioImaging Core in Ghent and Leuven is expert in correlative light and electron microscopy (CLEM) and volume electron microscopy. On the preclinical side, staff at ICMI in Brussels measure how (radio)pharmaceuticals distribute through the body, how long they stay and what dose tissues receive. MIRaCLe in Leuven combines radiopharmacy, nuclear medicine and image analysis, and takes new tracers from development through GMP production to preclinical and clinical validation.

Super-resolution microscopy is at AOMC in Diepenbeek and CIC in Leuven, while GLiM in Ghent and ACAM in Antwerp offer confocal and two-photon microscopy. Volume electron microscopy and CLEM are at the VIB BioImaging Core in Ghent and Leuven. Preclinical MRI, micro-CT, PET and SPECT are at Core ARTH Infinity in Ghent and the MICA-BIL Core Facility in Antwerp. ICMI in Brussels offers micro-PET/CT and micro-SPECT-CT. Clinical PET-MRI and PET/CT are at MIRaCLe in Leuven.

## Imaging technologies offered

Light microscopy is the largest part of the Node. Confocal microscopy gives sharp optical sections through cells and tissues, and two-photon microscopy reaches cells deep inside thick or living tissue. CIC offers stimulated emission depletion (STED) microscopy on an Abberior STED Expert line, and AOMC offers single-molecule localisation microscopy. Both resolve structures below the diffraction limit. Light-sheet microscopy at AOMC, ACAM and CIC images embryos, organs and other large specimens with little light exposure, and AOMC runs a Zeiss Lightsheet 7. Fluorescence lifetime imaging (FLIM), FRET and FRAP report molecular interactions, local environment and mobility. All three are at AOMC, GLiM and the VIB BioImaging Core. High-content screening at ACAM and the VIB BioImaging Core collects quantitative phenotypes across many wells, and intravital microscopy at CIC and ICMI follows cells inside living animals.

Preclinical imaging is the second large family. Small-animal MRI, micro-CT, PET and SPECT show anatomy, physiology and tracer distribution in living animals and allow repeat studies. The MICA-BIL Core Facility runs a Bruker 9.4T MRI with cryo-coil, and Core ARTH Infinity adds ultrasound with a VisualSonics Vevo 2100. MIRaCLe offers clinical PET-MRI and PET/CT alongside preclinical PET and SPECT. Volume electron microscopy is at the VIB BioImaging Core, which uses FIB-SEM and serial block-face SEM to reconstruct cells and tissues in three dimensions.

## Specialties and research focus

Much of the Node's work concerns cancer and the brain. MIRaCLe serves research in oncology, neurology, cardiology, and infection and inflammation, including Alzheimer's disease studies with tau and amyloid tracers. The Node's clinical PET tracers measure glucose metabolism, synaptic density, dopamine transporters, tau, neuroendocrine tumours and neuroinflammation. The MICA-BIL Core Facility focuses on neuroscience and oncology, and it also studies medical devices and materials.

ICMI works on the biodistribution, pharmacokinetics and dosimetry of (radio)pharmaceuticals and tests how well candidate therapies work in animal models. At CIC, research covers cell and tissue biology, neurophysiology and neurogastroenterology, with a strong interest in the enteric nervous system, which controls the gut. CIC images organs, tissue slices and organoids as well as single cells. The VIB BioImaging Core is embedded in the VIB centres for inflammation research and for brain and disease research, so its projects connect closely to those fields. GLiM supports projects across the life sciences, and ACAM works at scales from whole specimens down to the nanometre range.

## Services and user support

A project usually begins with methodological setup, where staff help design the study protocol and standard operating procedures. Support for obtaining preclinical and clinical ethical permission is part of this stage, and clinical studies also receive help with recruiting patients and subjects. Researchers are trained to use the instruments, with training workstations and a seminar room, or they work with technical assistance from staff.

For preparation, staff prepare probes and animals, and the Node provides animal facilities with housing and a choice of disease models. A BSL2 animal facility and an SPF facility for high-field MRI support animal work, and large animals such as mini-pigs can be scanned. Wet lab space and cell culture facilities at safety levels 1 and 2 are available for sample work. Autoradiography of ex vivo tissue complements in vivo studies with many isotopes. After acquisition, staff help with data processing and analysis and provide full clinical and preclinical quantitation for PET studies. Server space and data storage come with support for defining and implementing metadata.

Four facilities focus on light microscopy. GLiM supports life-science research with standard and specialised microscopes, from deconvolution widefield and spinning-disk confocal to photomanipulation and laser microdissection. AOMC at Hasselt University is a fluorescence microscopy core whose staff specialise in dynamic imaging, high-resolution imaging and light-sheet microscopy, and it offers single-molecule FRET and correlation spectroscopy. ACAM at the University of Antwerp works at the meso-, micro- and nanometre scale with a focus on quantitative systems microscopy and automation. CIC at KU Leuven, a recognised core since 2020, brings together scientists who use microscopy for biological and biomedical questions, from single molecules to organs and animals. It is the Node's main site for live multiphoton work and label-free harmonic generation imaging.

The VIB BioImaging Core works across two sites and combines light microscopy, functional imaging and volume electron microscopy with image analysis for life-science projects.

Three facilities concentrate on small animals. Core ARTH Infinity at Ghent University provides non-invasive in vivo imaging across MRI, nuclear, optical, CT and ultrasound methods. The MICA-BIL Core Facility joins the small-animal expertise of the Molecular Imaging Center Antwerp and the Bio-Imaging Lab. ICMI at Vrije Universiteit Brussel specialises in nuclear and optical small-animal imaging and offers medical image analysis. MIRaCLe, run by KU Leuven and UZ Leuven, links preclinical and clinical molecular imaging.
