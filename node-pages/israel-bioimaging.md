# Israel BioImaging

## Overview

Israel BioImaging links imaging across scales, from the nanoscale structure of cells and materials to whole living animals. It combines light microscopy, electron microscopy and preclinical imaging, and a dedicated facility leads each of these. The Node has three facilities at three institutions in Ramat Gan and Jerusalem, and it covers both biological and biomedical imaging.

Staff at the Kanbar Core Facility Light Microscopy Unit in Ramat Gan are experienced in confocal and live imaging, and they also offer image analysis. The team at CPM in Ramat Gan are electron microscopists who work with life-science specimens as well as semiconductors, thin films and two-dimensional materials. They support both academic and industrial projects. At the Wohl Institute for Translational Medicine at Hadassah in Jerusalem, staff specialise in preclinical molecular and medical imaging of mice and rats. They visualise and monitor disease progression in animal models, with experience in cancer, blood vessel growth, the brain and the heart.

Light microscopy, including confocal, total internal reflection fluorescence (TIRF) and STED super-resolution microscopy, is at the Kanbar Core Facility Light Microscopy Unit in Ramat Gan. High-content screening is also offered by that unit. Scanning electron microscopy with elemental analysis is at CPM in Ramat Gan, which also offers cryo-transmission electron microscopy. Preclinical imaging, including high-field MRI, PET and ultrasound, is at the Wohl Institute for Translational Medicine at Hadassah in Jerusalem. Image analysis is offered by the Kanbar Core Facility Light Microscopy Unit in Ramat Gan and the Wohl Institute for Translational Medicine at Hadassah in Jerusalem.

## Imaging technologies offered

At the Kanbar Core Facility Light Microscopy Unit, laser scanning confocal microscopy gives optical sections and multichannel 3D localisation of proteins and structures in cells and tissues. TIRF excites only a thin layer near the coverslip, so researchers can follow membranes and molecules at the cell surface with high contrast. STED resolves subcellular structures below the ordinary diffraction limit, and the unit runs a Leica SP8 STED for this work. High-content screening automates acquisition and analysis, so cell phenotypes can be compared across many wells and conditions.

CPM offers scanning electron microscopy, which shows the surface morphology of cells, tissues and materials. Its Magellan 400L high-resolution SEM is one example. Elemental analysis by energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) maps which elements are present in a region of the sample. Cryo-transmission electron microscopy adds near-native views of hydrated particles and thin cell regions.

The Wohl Institute for Translational Medicine at Hadassah images living mice and rats. Its cryogen-free 7T MRI carries a PET insert, so anatomy and tracer distribution are recorded together in the same session. PET/CT and micro-CT add 3D anatomy, including bone. A Vevo 3100 with LAZER-X combines ultrasound up to 70 MHz with photoacoustic imaging of blood vessels. In vivo optical imaging follows fluorescent and bioluminescent reporters in whole animals, and image analysis turns these data into quantitative results.

## Specialties and research focus

The research the Node serves spans marine biology, plant science, developmental biology, infectious disease, neuroscience, cancer and ageing. Its emphasis is on moving between scales, from cells to tissues and organs and on to whole-body imaging.

At the Wohl Institute for Translational Medicine at Hadassah, work centres on animal models of disease in mice and rats. Its application areas include cancer and the growth of new blood vessels, stem cells and development, and neurobiology and the brain. Orthopaedic and cardiac research are also part of its work. Because the animals are imaged while alive, researchers can return to the same model and follow how a disease progresses.

CPM serves both the life sciences and materials research. Alongside biological specimens, its staff image semiconductors, thin films and two-dimensional materials, and they work with academic and industrial groups. This mix means the facility handles very different specimens, from soft biological samples to engineered surfaces. The Kanbar Core Facility Light Microscopy Unit focuses on light microscopy and image analysis, and it brings this work to the cell and tissue level of the Node's scale range.

Two of the Node's facilities are at Bar-Ilan University in Ramat Gan, so light and electron microscopy sit at the same university.

CPM is the electron microscopy facility of the Bar-Ilan Institute for Nanotechnology and Advanced Materials (BINA). It offers scanning electron microscopy with elemental analysis, as well as cryo-transmission electron microscopy. Its staff are expert in electron microscopy for both biological specimens and materials such as semiconductors, thin films and two-dimensional materials. The facility works with academic and industrial users.

The Kanbar Core Facility Light Microscopy Unit is a core facility at Bar-Ilan University. It brings confocal and STED super-resolution microscopy together with TIRF and high-content screening. Its staff are expert in light microscopy and image analysis, and their work covers confocal, widefield and live imaging of biological samples.

The Wohl Institute for Translational Medicine at Hadassah, at Hadassah Ein Kerem in Jerusalem, is the Node's preclinical imaging facility. It visualises and monitors disease progression in mice and rats with magnetic resonance, nuclear, X-ray, acoustic and optical methods. Its staff are expert in preclinical molecular and medical imaging. They combine modalities such as PET with MRI or CT in the same animal, and they also offer image analysis.
