# Israel BioImaging

## Overview

Israel BioImaging links imaging across scales, from molecules in single cells to whole living animals. Its three facilities cover light microscopy, electron microscopy and preclinical in vivo imaging, so a project can move between methods as its question changes in scale. The Node brings together three institutions in two cities and serves both biological and biomedical research. Two facilities belong to Bar-Ilan University in Ramat Gan, and the third is at Hadassah Ein Kerem in Jerusalem.

Each facility brings its own area of expertise. Staff at the Kanbar Core Facility Light Microscopy Unit in Ramat Gan specialise in fluorescence light microscopy and image analysis, from confocal live imaging to super-resolution STED and automated screening. The CPM team in Ramat Gan, part of the Bar-Ilan Institute for Nanotechnology and Advanced Materials (BINA), works in electron microscopy. It handles biological specimens as well as thin films, semiconductors and 2D materials. At the Wohl Institute for Translational Medicine at Hadassah in Jerusalem, staff are experienced in preclinical molecular and medical imaging of mice and rats. Their work centres on visualising and monitoring disease progression in animal models.

Cell biologists who need to resolve subcellular structures, or to measure phenotypes across many conditions, will find most support at the Kanbar Core Facility Light Microscopy Unit. Projects that need fine surface and structural detail from electron microscopy, including work where biology meets materials science, suit CPM. Researchers using rodent models of cancer, vascular growth or brain disorders should look to the Wohl Institute for Translational Medicine at Hadassah, where several in vivo methods can be combined in the same animals.

## Imaging technologies offered

Preclinical imaging is the broadest family at the Node, and all of it is offered at the Wohl Institute for Translational Medicine at Hadassah. Researchers can combine micro-PET, which measures where a tracer goes and how fast, with high-field micro-MRI at 7 T or micro-CT for 3D anatomy including bone. Micro-ultrasound and photoacoustic imaging show organs, vessels and blood supply without ionising radiation. In vivo optical imaging follows fluorescent or bioluminescent reporters in the intact animal over repeated sessions. Instruments include a 7T cryogen-free MRI with a SiPM PET insert and a Vevo 3100 with LAZER-X for photoacoustic work.

Light microscopy is offered at the Kanbar Core Facility Light Microscopy Unit. Laser scanning confocal microscopy gives optical sections and multichannel 3D localisation, while TIRF microscopy picks out membranes and molecules close to the coverslip. Stimulated emission depletion (STED) microscopy on a Leica SP8 STED resolves subcellular structures below the diffraction limit. High-content screening measures cell phenotypes across many wells. Electron microscopy at CPM centres on scanning electron microscopy (SEM), which shows surface morphology in fine detail on instruments such as the Magellan 400L HR-SEM. CPM also offers cryo-TEM and elemental analysis by EDX. Image analysis is offered at both the Kanbar unit and the Wohl Institute.

## Specialties and research focus

Across the Node, the research supported spans marine biology, plant science and developmental biology, as well as infectious disease, neuroscience, cancer and ageing. The facilities divide this work by scale, from specimens under the microscope to whole animals.

The Wohl Institute for Translational Medicine at Hadassah works with mice and rats across several disease areas. Its research fields include cancer and the growth of new blood vessels, stem cells and development, and the brain and wider neurobiology. Cardiac and orthopaedic questions are also part of its work. Because the institute focuses on visualising and monitoring disease in animal models, it suits studies that follow the same animals as disease progresses or a treatment takes effect.

CPM works on both sides of the line between life and materials science. Alongside biological specimens, it images semiconductors, thin films and 2D materials. This makes it a natural partner for groups whose questions involve both biological and engineered samples. The Kanbar Core Facility Light Microscopy Unit concentrates on light microscopy and image analysis, and its staff combine the two within a single project.

## Services and user support

A project at the Kanbar Core Facility Light Microscopy Unit can start with methodological setup, where staff help adapt an imaging approach to the research question. The unit also prepares samples, including staining, and gives technical assistance while researchers run the instruments. The Node offers training in image acquisition and training in image processing and analysis, so visiting researchers can learn to collect and handle their own data. Once images are acquired, the Kanbar unit offers data processing and analysis.

CPM works more as an imaging service, with staff acquiring the images and then processing and analysing the data. CPM also offers fabrication services alongside its imaging work. At the Wohl Institute for Translational Medicine at Hadassah, staff give technical assistance with implementing imaging technologies, which helps groups bring preclinical imaging into their studies.

### Services offered*

- Methodological setup (at Kanbar Light Microscopy Unit)
- Sample preparation (including staining), at Kanbar Light Microscopy Unit
- Technical assistance to run instrument (at Kanbar Light Microscopy Unit)
- Training in image acquisition
- Training in image processing and analysis
- Data processing and analysis (at Kanbar Light Microscopy Unit)
- Imaging services (at CPM-BINA)
- Data processing and analysis (at CPM-BINA)
- Fabrication services (at CPM-BINA)
- Technical assistance (imaging technology implementation), at Wohl Institute for Translational Medicine

The Node has two facilities in Ramat Gan and one in Jerusalem. CPM belongs to BINA, the Bar-Ilan Institute for Nanotechnology and Advanced Materials. It is equipped with scanning and transmission electron microscopes for imaging, analysis and fabrication, and it serves both the materials and the life sciences.

The Kanbar Core Facility Light Microscopy Unit is a core facility at Bar-Ilan University. It supports confocal and live imaging, and it offers image analysis within the same unit, so acquisition and quantification can be planned together. Because the two Bar-Ilan facilities share a university, a project can begin with fluorescence imaging at the Kanbar unit and continue with electron microscopy at CPM without leaving Ramat Gan.

The Wohl Institute for Translational Medicine at Hadassah is the Node's in vivo facility. It provides imaging infrastructure for visualising and monitoring disease progression in animal models, and it brings several preclinical modalities together in one place. A study can therefore pair molecular readouts from PET or optical imaging with anatomical or vascular imaging in the same animals. The institute complements the Ramat Gan facilities by taking questions from cells and specimens up to the whole organism. Image analysis is offered both at the Wohl Institute and at the Kanbar unit.
