# Madrid Advanced Microscopy Center (MAdMiC)

## Overview

MAdMiC is a Euro-BioImaging Node in Madrid that mainly offers advanced light microscopy, with a focus on live samples and dynamic processes in cells and tissues. The Node is built around cross-scale imaging, and it links these optical methods with cryo-electron microscopy and with optical imaging of living mice. It has four facilities at two institutions in Madrid, the Centro de Biología Molecular Severo Ochoa (CBM) and the Centro Nacional de Biotecnología (CNB), and covers both biological and biomedical imaging.

Staff at the two light microscopy facilities work with fixed and live cells and tissues, and they are experienced in functional microscopy and super-resolution imaging. The team at the Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility at CNB in Madrid has expertise in STED nanoscopy, single-molecule localisation and multidimensional live-cell imaging. SMOA at CBM in Madrid combines advanced optical microscopy with image analysis, and its staff also handle immunohistochemistry and colour imaging. The Cryo-Electron Microscopy Facility at CNB in Madrid brings expertise in structural biology and in cryo-correlative microscopy of biological material. The Preclinical Biomedicine Facility at CBM in Madrid works with mice, and its staff support experimental design, in vivo imaging and metabolic phenotyping.

Confocal, STED and light-sheet microscopy are at both SMOA and the Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility. These two facilities also both offer functional methods such as FLIM, FRET and FRAP. TIRF, single-molecule localisation and fluorescence correlation spectroscopy are at the Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility, while spinning disk confocal and image scanning microscopy are at SMOA. Cryo-electron microscopy methods and correlative light and electron microscopy (CLEM) are at the Cryo-Electron Microscopy Facility. In vivo optical imaging of mice is at the Preclinical Biomedicine Facility.

## Imaging technologies offered

Most of the Node's methods are fluorescence light microscopy. Laser scanning and spinning disk confocal microscopy give optical sections of cells and tissues for 3D, multichannel localisation, and deconvolution widefield and image scanning microscopy add sharper images of subcellular structures. For detail below the diffraction limit, STED is offered at SMOA and the Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility, and single-molecule localisation microscopy at the Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility maps the positions of individual molecules. Light-sheet microscopy images embryos, organs and cleared specimens while exciting only the plane being recorded.

Functional methods measure what molecules do as well as where they are. FLIM reports on the molecular environment, FRET detects proteins within nanometres of each other and FRAP measures how quickly molecules move and exchange. Fluorescence correlation spectroscopy at the Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility measures diffusion and concentration directly. Instruments include Leica Stellaris 8 systems with TauSTEDXtend, FLIM and FCS, and an Abbelight SAFe 360, both at the Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility, and a Zeiss Lightsheet Z7 at SMOA.

At the Cryo-Electron Microscopy Facility, cryo-fluorescence microscopy locates fluorescent targets in vitrified samples, and correlative light and electron microscopy then places them in their ultrastructural context. A Zeiss CrossBeam 550 cryo-FIB-SEM thins frozen cells for further electron imaging. The Preclinical Biomedicine Facility offers in vivo optical imaging, which follows fluorescent or bioluminescent reporters in intact mice over time. Image analysis is part of the work at both light microscopy facilities.

## Specialties and research focus

The Node aims to address biological questions across scales, from molecules and cells to whole animals, and it gives particular weight to live samples and dynamic studies. Its facilities serve cell biology, structural biology and preclinical biomedicine.

The Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility at CNB supports cellular and tissue fluorescence imaging of both fixed and live samples. Its research applications centre on functional microscopy and super-resolution work, which researchers use to study molecular organisation and behaviour inside cells. SMOA at CBM also works with live and fixed samples, and its applications include immunohistochemistry and colour imaging of tissue.

The Cryo-Electron Microscopy Facility at CNB serves structural biology. Its work concerns the cryogenic analysis of biological material, where molecular signals are related to the ultrastructure around them. The Preclinical Biomedicine Facility at CBM works with mouse models, and its research focus is metabolism and in vivo imaging. Alongside imaging, the facility carries out metabolic phenotyping and haematology and biochemistry analysis in these mice.

## Services and user support

A project starts with methodological set-up and experimental design, where staff help write the study protocol and standard operating procedures. Sample preparation covers common methods such as immunofluorescence and immunohistochemistry as well as advanced ones such as preparation for STORM and tissue clearing. Researchers can be trained in sample preparation and in using the instruments, or staff can run the instruments and acquire images for them.

Visiting researchers have wet lab space with glassware, common reagents, small lab equipment and fridges and freezers for sample storage. Cell culture facilities at safety level 1 are available, and the Node supplies common microscopy reagents such as antibodies, dyes, mounting media and imaging chambers. Other instruments support the imaging work, including incubators, a vibratome, a microfluidics system, metabolic cages and a mouse treadmill. For mouse work the Node offers animal preparation, animal facilities and housing. Seminar rooms are also available.

After acquisition, staff help with image visualisation, pre-processing and analysis, and they also train researchers to do this themselves on the image analysis workstations. Support continues through data reporting and preparing images for publication, and up to 100 TB of server space is available for data.

Two facilities provide the Node's light microscopy. The Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility at CNB is a service unit for advanced fluorescence microscopy of fixed and live samples. Its expertise spans confocal and functional microscopy, STED and single-molecule localisation super-resolution, light-sheet mesoscopy and multidimensional live-cell imaging, together with the image analysis that follows. SMOA, the Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility at CBM, gives advice, technical support and training in advanced optical microscopy and image analysis. It offers confocal, STED, light-sheet and functional methods, and it also supplies microscopy-related reagents.

The Cryo-Electron Microscopy Facility at CNB is a core facility for structural biology. It prepares samples and acquires data under cryogenic conditions, and its staff are expert in cryo-correlative workflows that link a fluorescent signal to the structure around it. Its methods include cryo-fluorescence microscopy, live-cell and cryo CLEM, cryo-SEM and cryo-focused ion beam milling, which makes thick frozen specimens thin enough for electron imaging.

The Preclinical Biomedicine Facility at CBM is a core facility for experimental work with mice. Its staff help design preclinical studies and carry out in vivo optical imaging. The facility combines this imaging with metabolic phenotyping and with haematology and biochemistry analysis, so imaging data sit alongside physiological measurements from the same mouse models.
