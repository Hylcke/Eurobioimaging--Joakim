# Madrid Advanced Microscopy Center (MAdMiC)

## Overview

MAdMiC is a Euro-BioImaging Node in Madrid, and its main strength is advanced fluorescence light microscopy of live and fixed samples. Its two optical facilities combine confocal, super-resolution and functional methods, so researchers can see how molecules move, interact and organise inside cells and tissues. The Node joins four facilities at two research institutions in the city, the Centro de Biología Molecular Severo Ochoa (CBM) and the Centro Nacional de Biotecnología (CNB). SMOA and the Preclinical Biomedicine Facility belong to CBM, while the Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility and the Cryo-Electron Microscopy Facility belong to CNB. Together they cover biological and biomedical imaging, from vitrified cells to living mice.

The Node aims at cross-scale imaging, with particular attention to live samples and dynamic processes. Staff at SMOA bring expertise in advanced optical microscopy and image analysis for both live and fixed material, and they support immunohistochemistry and colour imaging. The team at the Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility works with multidimensional live-cell imaging, nanoscopy and fluorescence fluctuation measurements. At the Cryo-Electron Microscopy Facility, staff prepare and image biological material at cryogenic temperature for structural biology, including cryo-correlative work. The Preclinical Biomedicine Facility helps design experiments with mice, and its staff are experienced in in vivo imaging, metabolic phenotyping and blood analysis.

Cell biologists who need super-resolution or want to measure molecular mobility and interactions will find most support in the two optical facilities, which share many of their methods. Projects that need single-molecule localisation or correlation spectroscopy should look to the Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility at CNB. Structural biologists who want to locate a fluorescent signal in frozen cells before electron microscopy should turn to the Cryo-Electron Microscopy Facility. Studies that follow reporters in living mice belong at the Preclinical Biomedicine Facility at CBM.

## Imaging technologies offered

Most of the Node's methods are fluorescence light microscopy, and SMOA and the Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility share the core of them. Both offer laser scanning confocal and deconvolution widefield microscopy for multichannel 3D imaging of cells and tissues. Both also run light-sheet microscopy, which images embryos, organs and other large specimens with little unnecessary light exposure. SMOA adds spinning disk confocal and image scanning microscopy, which suit fast or gentle imaging of living cells. For detail below the diffraction limit, both facilities offer stimulated emission depletion (STED) microscopy. The Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility adds single-molecule localisation microscopy (SMLM) and TIRF microscopy for molecules near the coverslip.

Functional imaging is the second strength. Both optical facilities measure molecular proximity with fluorescence lifetime imaging (FLIM) and fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET), and they follow molecular exchange with fluorescence recovery after photobleaching (FRAP). Fluorescence correlation spectroscopy at the Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility measures molecular diffusion and concentration. Its instruments include Leica STELLARIS 8 systems with TauSTEDXtend, FLIM and FCS capabilities and an Abbelight SAFe 360. SMOA runs a Zeiss Lightsheet Z7.

The Cryo-Electron Microscopy Facility offers cryo fluorescence microscopy and live-cell correlative light and electron microscopy, which link a fluorescent signal or a live event to its ultrastructure. The Preclinical Biomedicine Facility provides in vivo optical imaging, which reads fluorescent or bioluminescent reporters repeatedly in intact mice.

## Specialties and research focus

MAdMiC's research focus follows its aim of cross-scale imaging, so a question can move from molecular structure through cells and tissues to the whole animal. The Node has a particular interest in live samples and in processes that change over time. At SMOA, projects involve both live and fixed samples, and immunohistochemistry with colour imaging is part of the work. The Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility at CNB serves cellular and tissue studies in fixed and living material, especially those that need functional measurements or super-resolution. These two facilities therefore support questions about how cells organise their components and how molecules behave inside them.

At the finest scale, the Cryo-Electron Microscopy Facility serves structural biology and the cryogenic analysis of biological material. At the largest scale, the Preclinical Biomedicine Facility works with mouse models. Its research areas cover metabolism and in vivo imaging rather than one particular disease.

## Services and user support

Projects usually begin with help on methodological setup and experimental design, such as writing a study protocol and standard operating procedures. Staff then assist with sample preparation, from common methods like immunofluorescence and immunohistochemistry to advanced ones such as STORM labelling and tissue clearing. Researchers can have staff run the instruments and acquire images, or be trained to prepare samples and use the infrastructure themselves.

The Node provides wet lab space with glassware, common reagents, small equipment and fridge and freezer storage, as well as cell culture facilities at safety level 1. Supporting equipment includes incubators, a vibratome and a microfluidics system. Common microscopy reagents are supplied, from antibodies and dyes to mounting media and imaging chambers. For mouse work, the Node offers animal preparation, animal facilities and housing, together with metabolic cages and a mouse treadmill. After acquisition, staff help with visualisation, pre-processing and analysis, train researchers in these steps and support data reporting and image publication. Researchers can use image analysis workstations and up to 100 TB of server space.

The Node is organised around two institutions, each with its own optical microscopy facility. At CBM, SMOA in Madrid gives advice, technical support and training in advanced optical microscopy and image analysis, and it supplies microscopy reagents. The Preclinical Biomedicine Facility in Madrid, also at CBM, supports experimental design and imaging work with mice. This means cellular findings from SMOA can be followed in whole animals at the same institute.

At CNB, the Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility in Madrid is a service unit for advanced fluorescence microscopy of fixed and live samples, from confocal to super-resolution and functional work. The Cryo-Electron Microscopy Facility in Madrid, also at CNB, is a core facility that prepares samples and acquires data at cryogenic temperature. Because both belong to CNB, a project can combine advanced light microscopy with cryo-correlative or live-cell correlative work without leaving the institute.

The two optical facilities overlap in confocal, light-sheet, STED and functional imaging, and both offer image analysis. They differ at the edges. SMOA offers spinning disk and image scanning microscopy, while the Advanced Optical Microscopy Facility offers single-molecule localisation, TIRF and correlation spectroscopy.
