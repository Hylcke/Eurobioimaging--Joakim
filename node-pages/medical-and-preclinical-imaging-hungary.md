# Medical and Preclinical Imaging Hungary

## Overview

Medical and Preclinical Imaging Hungary is a biomedical imaging Node built around in vivo imaging of animal models, with nuclear imaging at its centre. Its preclinical methods include micro-PET and micro-SPECT, which follow radioactive tracers in living animals, and both are offered alone and combined with CT or MRI. The Node also covers clinical and human imaging, so its work spans the step from animal studies to studies in people. Most of its methods belong to preclinical imaging, with a smaller group of human PET techniques alongside them.

The Node describes its approach as "Molecules through Mice to Man", or M3. The idea is an integrated imaging pipeline that runs from molecular-level characterisation through preclinical imaging to human clinical studies. Tracer imaging in animals and tracer imaging in people therefore sit within the same Node, which helps when a readout developed in mouse models needs to move towards the clinic. Alongside nuclear imaging, the preclinical methods include high-field magnetic resonance, X-ray micro-CT, ultrasound and optical imaging of small animals.

Projects that develop or test a radiotracer and want to follow it from animal models into human imaging make the most of what this Node combines. Researchers who study disease models over time can pair tracer imaging with anatomy from micro-CT or soft-tissue contrast from MRI at 7 tesla or more. Groups working with fixed tissue can use ex-vivo micro-CT and MRI to examine specimens in three dimensions without cutting sections.

## Imaging technologies offered

Nuclear imaging is the Node's strongest family. Micro-PET and micro-SPECT measure where a radioactive tracer goes in a living animal and how its distribution changes over time. SPECT works with gamma-emitting isotopes, so it opens a different set of tracers from PET. Both are offered in hybrid form as micro-PET/CT, micro-SPECT-CT and micro-PET/MRI, which place the tracer signal on an anatomical image. The CT component also allows the PET signal to be corrected for attenuation.

For anatomy and physiology without tracers, the Node offers micro-MRI and MR spectroscopy at fields of 7 tesla or more, along with micro-CT and micro-ultrasound. MRI gives soft-tissue contrast, and spectroscopy adds information on tissue chemistry. Micro-CT shows mineralised structures such as bone in three dimensions, while ultrasound gives quick structural and flow measurements without ionising radiation. In vivo optical imaging reads out fluorescent or bioluminescent reporters across the whole animal, and laser speckle imaging maps relative blood flow at accessible surfaces. Micro-CT and high-field MRI can also be applied ex vivo to fixed specimens.

On the human side, the Node offers PET and PET-MRI, together with PET/CT, for measuring tracer distribution in human organs and tissues.

## Specialties and research focus

The Node's research focus follows from its M3 concept, which links molecular characterisation, preclinical imaging and human clinical studies in one pipeline. This makes it most relevant to translational questions, where a finding in animal models needs to be carried towards studies in people. As the name of the concept suggests, mouse models form the preclinical middle of that pipeline.

The Node works with three kinds of sample. Living animals are imaged repeatedly with nuclear, magnetic resonance, X-ray, ultrasound and optical methods. Fixed organs and tissue specimens are examined ex vivo with micro-CT and high-field MRI. Human subjects are imaged with PET and its hybrid forms, which completes the route from molecule to patient.

## Facilities

Medical and Preclinical Imaging Hungary brings preclinical and clinical imaging together within one national Node. Its preclinical side covers small-animal nuclear imaging, magnetic resonance, X-ray CT, ultrasound and optical imaging, together with ex-vivo work on specimens. Its clinical side covers human PET and its hybrid forms with MRI and CT.

The two sides are designed to work as one pipeline rather than as separate services. A tracer or imaging readout can be characterised in animal models and then taken into human PET without leaving the Node. Researchers planning this kind of translational project can use the contact details on this page to reach the Node and find the right site for each stage.
