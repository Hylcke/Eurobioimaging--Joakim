# Medical and Preclinical Imaging Hungary

## Overview

Medical and Preclinical Imaging Hungary is a biomedical imaging Node that mainly offers preclinical imaging of animal models. Its core is nuclear imaging with micro-PET and micro-SPECT, often combined with CT or MRI so that tracer signals can be placed in their anatomical context. The Node also offers human imaging with PET and PET-MRI. It works in Hungary and covers both preclinical and clinical biomedical imaging.

The Node organises its work around what it calls the Molecules through Mice to Man (M3) concept. This connects the characterisation of molecules, imaging in animal models and clinical studies in people into one imaging pipeline. Its expertise therefore lies in translational imaging, in which an imaging approach studied in mice is carried forward to human PET. Staff combine tracer imaging with anatomical, functional and spectroscopic readouts in the same animals.

Preclinical nuclear imaging includes micro-PET and micro-SPECT, together with the hybrid systems micro-PET/CT, micro-PET/MRI and micro-SPECT-CT. Anatomical and physiological imaging of animals uses micro-CT and micro-MRI/MRS at 7 tesla or above. Micro-ultrasound and in vivo optical imaging complete the preclinical set. Ex-vivo micro-CT and ex-vivo micro-MRI/MRS apply the same methods to excised organs and specimens, while human imaging centres on PET and PET-MRI.

## Imaging technologies offered

Most of the Node's methods serve preclinical imaging. Micro-PET and micro-SPECT measure where a radioactive tracer goes in a living animal and how its distribution changes over time. Micro-PET/CT and micro-SPECT-CT add an X-ray scan, which locates the tracer within the anatomy. Micro-PET/MRI pairs the tracer signal with the soft-tissue contrast and physiological measures of magnetic resonance.

On its own, micro-MRI/MRS at 7 tesla or above images organs and tissues in animals and measures their chemical make-up through spectroscopy, without sectioning. Micro-CT gives three-dimensional anatomy and shows mineralised structures such as bone. Micro-ultrasound records structure and blood flow in organs and accessible vessels without ionising radiation. In vivo optical imaging follows fluorescent or bioluminescent reporters across the whole animal, so the same reporter can be measured repeatedly in one subject. Laser speckle imaging maps relative changes in blood flow at the surface of tissue.

For specimens, ex-vivo micro-CT and ex-vivo micro-MRI/MRS image excised organs and tissues in three dimensions. In human imaging, PET measures tracer distribution and kinetics in organs and tissues, and PET-MRI combines this with MR soft-tissue information. PET/CT adds the anatomical context of an X-ray scan to human tracer studies.

## Specialties and research focus

The Node's research focus is translational imaging. Its M3 concept, Molecules through Mice to Man, links three stages of a study. Work starts with molecular-level characterisation, continues with imaging in animal models and leads on to clinical studies in people.

This focus shapes the questions the Node supports. Researchers follow the behaviour of tracers and imaging agents in mice, and the same kind of measurement continues in human PET and PET-MRI. The Node's combination of preclinical and clinical imaging serves biomedical research that needs results in animal models to connect with observations in patients.

## Facilities

The Node's work falls into two parts. Preclinical imaging covers nuclear, magnetic resonance, X-ray, ultrasound and optical methods for animal models, as well as ex-vivo imaging of organs and specimens. Clinical imaging covers PET and PET-MRI in people. The people behind both parts share the M3 approach, so their expertise lies in carrying imaging studies from molecules and mice through to human subjects.
