# Phase Contrast Imaging Flagship Node Trieste

## Overview

The Phase Contrast Imaging Flagship Node Trieste offers X-ray phase-contrast imaging and tomography with synchrotron radiation, mainly for soft biological tissues. Its work centres on the SYRMEP beamline at the Elettra synchrotron, where phase-contrast methods reveal soft-tissue detail that ordinary X-ray absorption imaging shows poorly. The Node has a single facility, the SYRMEP beamline in Trieste (Basovizza), hosted by Elettra-Sincrotrone Trieste S.C.p.A., and it works in biomedical imaging.

Staff at the SYRMEP beamline have many years of experience in X-ray imaging techniques and in image processing and analysis. They built this experience through collaboration with other imaging groups. The team has also developed low-dose phase-contrast computed tomography (CT) for in vivo studies, together with expertise in dosimetry, which measures the radiation dose a sample receives. Staff tailor imaging and reconstruction protocols to each sample by choosing among different pre-processing and reconstruction algorithms. Their experience with soft tissue includes virtual histology of cancer, tissue around dental implants and an atlas of the human brainstem.

The SYRMEP beamline combines the Node's two groups of methods in one place. Synchrotron phase-contrast imaging runs on the beamline itself, which images samples at several scales. Ex-vivo micro-CT is offered in TomoLab, the facility's auxiliary micro-CT laboratory, which uses a conventional X-ray source rather than the synchrotron beam.

## Imaging technologies offered

Phase Contrast Imaging (PCI) is the Node's main method. It records how X-rays are bent as they pass through a sample, and not only how much they are absorbed. This gives contrast in soft tissues such as brain, tumours or the tissue around an implant, which absorb X-rays too weakly to show well in conventional images. Combined with tomography, PCI produces three-dimensional views of tissue architecture without cutting the sample.

The SYRMEP beamline works in propagation-based phase-contrast mode at pixel sizes from 1 µm to 50 µm, depending on the sample. This allows a researcher to move from a whole organ to fine structural detail. A dedicated Analyzer-Based Imaging setup uses a monochromatic beam between 15 keV and 35 keV and offers a second way to separate phase effects from absorption. Reconstruction is adjusted to each data set through different pre-processing and reconstruction algorithms in the STP software. The experimental station can also be adapted for in-situ studies and for testing new instrumentation.

Ex-vivo micro-CT complements the beamline. The TomoLab micro-focus X-ray micro-CT system measures X-ray absorption and shows three-dimensional anatomy, including mineralised structures such as bone and teeth, without physical serial sectioning.

## Specialties and research focus

The SYRMEP beamline works mainly on biomedical and soft-tissue imaging. In cancer research, phase-contrast tomography serves as virtual histology, which shows tumour tissue in three dimensions without slicing it into sections. Dental research at the beamline looks at the peri-implant tissue that surrounds dental implants, where bone and soft tissue meet. Staff have also contributed imaging to an atlas of the human brainstem, a project that depends on clear contrast between closely packed soft structures.

The facility's research scope reaches beyond medicine. SYRMEP also images samples from botany and zoology, so plant and animal specimens form part of its work. Food science, materials research and geology use the same tomographic methods to look inside samples without damaging them. Cultural heritage objects are imaged at the beamline as well, because many of them cannot be cut or sampled.

This mix means the staff work with specimens that range from fixed human and animal tissue to plant material and non-biological objects. The biomedical work, and soft tissue in particular, remains the centre of the Node's research.

## Services and user support

A project at the Node starts with methodological setup. Staff help prepare the experimental proposal, optimise the imaging protocol for the sample and agree the operating procedures for the beamtime. For projects that involve in vivo studies, the team also supports researchers with the paperwork for ethics permissions.

Alongside the synchrotron beamline, researchers have access to TomoLab, the auxiliary conventional benchtop micro-CT system. This allows samples to be scanned with a laboratory X-ray source as part of the same project. Visiting groups can also use the Elettra users' support laboratories and the Biology Lab, which gives them space to handle and prepare their samples on site.

Once the scans are complete, support continues with the data. Staff give advice on processing and analysing the tomographic data sets, drawing on their background in image analysis. They also assist with advanced visualisation and rendering of the reconstructed CT data, which helps researchers explore three-dimensional structures and prepare images for presentation and publication.

The SYRMEP beamline in Trieste (Basovizza) is the Node's only facility and is part of the Elettra synchrotron, run by Elettra-Sincrotrone Trieste S.C.p.A. It offers Phase Contrast Imaging with synchrotron radiation, aimed particularly at soft biological tissues, and ex-vivo micro-CT in its auxiliary TomoLab laboratory. The staff are experts in micro-CT and synchrotron tomographic imaging, and they combine this with long experience in image processing and analysis.

The team's methodological work includes low-dose phase-contrast CT and dosimetry, two areas that matter whenever radiation dose has to be kept low. The beamline has a control interface designed for researchers without synchrotron-specific expertise, so biologists and clinicians can follow and steer their own experiments. Its experimental station is flexible and supports in-situ studies as well as the installation of new instrumentation.

The facility serves biomedical research on soft tissue, from cancer virtual histology to dental peri-implant tissue and the human brainstem. It also works with botany, zoology, food, cultural heritage, materials and geology.
