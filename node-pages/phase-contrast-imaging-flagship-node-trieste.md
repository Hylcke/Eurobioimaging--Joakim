# Phase Contrast Imaging Flagship Node Trieste

## Overview

The Phase Contrast Imaging Flagship Node Trieste offers synchrotron X-ray phase-contrast imaging of biological soft tissue in three dimensions. Its strength lies with samples that give little contrast in ordinary X-ray images, so whole organs and tissue blocks can be examined without cutting them into sections. The Node is a single facility, the SYRMEP beamline at Elettra-Sincrotrone Trieste S.C.p.A. in Trieste (Basovizza). It works in biomedical imaging, and alongside the synchrotron beamline it runs TomoLab, a laboratory micro-CT system for ex-vivo samples.

The SYRMEP staff have many years of experience in X-ray imaging and in image processing and analysis, built up through collaboration with other imaging groups. They have developed low-dose phase-contrast computed tomography (CT), which keeps the radiation dose low enough to suit in vivo studies. They also have expertise in dosimetry, the measurement of the dose a sample receives. The team tailors imaging and reconstruction protocols to each sample by choosing among different pre-processing and reconstruction algorithms. The beamline control interface is designed for researchers without synchrotron-specific expertise.

The Node suits projects that need a three-dimensional view of soft tissue at microscopic scale. Cancer researchers can use the beamline for virtual histology, looking through tumour tissue in 3D without physical sectioning. Dental and neuroscience groups will find experience with peri-implant tissue and with mapping the human brainstem. Projects that combine soft tissue with bone or teeth make the most of the facility, because the beamline and TomoLab sit together at SYRMEP.

## Imaging technologies offered

Phase Contrast Imaging (PCI) is the Node's core method, and the SYRMEP beamline offers it. Ordinary X-ray imaging relies on how much a sample absorbs, which tells soft tissues apart poorly. PCI also records how the beam is bent as it passes through the sample, so boundaries between soft tissues become visible. Researchers can see the architecture of organs and tissue specimens in 3D and follow structures through the whole sample.

The SYRMEP beamline works in propagation-based phase-contrast mode at pixel sizes from 1 µm to 50 µm, depending on the sample. This lets a project move from an overview of a large specimen to fine detail in a smaller one. A dedicated Analyzer-Based Imaging setup uses a monochromatic beam between 15 keV and 35 keV and gives a second route to phase contrast. Reconstruction protocols can be adjusted for each sample through the STP software, and the experimental station is flexible enough to host in-situ studies and new instrumentation.

Ex-vivo micro-CT complements the synchrotron work and is offered by the SYRMEP beamline on the TomoLab micro-focus X-ray micro-CT system. This conventional attenuation tomography shows preclinical anatomy and specimen structure in 3D, and it is well suited to mineralised structures such as bone and teeth.

## Specialties and research focus

Research at the SYRMEP beamline centres on biomedical and soft-tissue imaging. Cancer studies use the beamline for virtual histology, where tumour tissue is examined in three dimensions rather than slice by slice on glass. Dental research looks at the tissue around implants, where soft tissue meets bone and implant material in one sample. Neuroanatomy is another focus, with work on an atlas of the human brainstem.

The facility also serves research well beyond medicine. Its application areas include botany and zoology, so plant and animal specimens are part of its work. Food science, cultural heritage, materials science and geology use the same imaging, which reflects how widely 3D X-ray imaging of internal structure can be applied. For a life scientist, this breadth means the staff are used to adapting protocols to unfamiliar specimens. The common thread across these fields is a question about internal structure that cannot be answered from the surface of a sample. It also lies in samples that would be damaged or lost by physical sectioning, such as rare human tissue or valuable specimens from collections.

## Services and user support

Work with the Node begins with methodological set-up. Staff help prepare the proposal, optimise the imaging protocol and plan the operating procedures, so that the beam settings and pixel size match the scientific question. For in vivo studies, the team supports the paperwork needed for ethical permissions.

During the experiment, researchers can use the Elettra users' support laboratories and the Biology Lab to handle and prepare their samples on site. Alongside the synchrotron beamline, the SYRMEP beamline gives access to TomoLab, its auxiliary benchtop micro-CT system. TomoLab can be used for conventional micro-CT of samples that do not need synchrotron radiation, or alongside beamline measurements.

Once the tomographic data are reconstructed, staff assist with advanced visualisation and rendering, which turns the 3D datasets into views that show the structures of interest. The team also gives support and advice on data processing and analysis, drawing on its long experience with X-ray image analysis.

### Services offered

- Access to TOMOLAB, the available auxiliary microCT conventional benchtop system
- Support for ethic permissions paperwork, in case of in-vivo studies
- Methodological setup (assistance for proposals preparation, optimization of imaging protocol and operation procedures)
- Assistance for advanced visualization and rendering of the reconstructed CT data
- Support/advice for data processing and analysis
- Access to the Elettra users’ support Labs and Biology Lab

The SYRMEP beamline at Elettra-Sincrotrone Trieste S.C.p.A. in Trieste (Basovizza) is the Node's only facility. It is a beamline at the Elettra synchrotron, where the high-quality X-ray beam makes phase-contrast imaging of soft biological tissue possible. The facility suits projects that need detailed 3D images of whole specimens without destroying them, from tumour samples to brain tissue and dental implants.

TomoLab, a laboratory micro-focus micro-CT system, sits alongside the beamline within the same facility. It covers conventional attenuation micro-CT, so a project can run preliminary or complementary scans in the lab and reserve the synchrotron for questions that need phase contrast. Samples with mineralised structures can be imaged on TomoLab, while their soft-tissue surroundings are studied at the beamline.

Because one team runs both systems, imaging protocols, reconstruction and analysis follow a single approach from planning to final data. The same staff also bring the experience in low-dose phase-contrast CT and dosimetry that in vivo work requires. The experimental station can be adapted for in-situ studies and for testing new instrumentation.
