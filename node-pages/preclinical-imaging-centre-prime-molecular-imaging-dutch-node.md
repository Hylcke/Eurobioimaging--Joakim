# Preclinical Imaging Centre (PRIME) - Molecular Imaging Dutch Node

## Overview

The PRIME Molecular Imaging Dutch Node offers preclinical imaging of small animals, mainly mice and rats. Most of its methods are at the Preclinical Imaging Centre (PRIME), which combines magnetic resonance, nuclear, X-ray, ultrasound and optical imaging for longitudinal and multimodal studies. The Node has two facilities, PRIME at Radboudumc in Nijmegen and the Hubrecht Imaging Center at the Hubrecht Institute in Utrecht, and it covers biomedical imaging.

PRIME is housed at the Central Animal Laboratory of Radboudumc. A multidisciplinary team runs it, with imaging experts from radiology and nuclear medicine. The staff radiolabel radiopharmaceuticals and test them in vitro and in vivo, alongside their PET and SPECT imaging. They also work on photodynamic therapy and on behaviour and cognition studies in rodents, and they image human tissue ex vivo as well as small animals. At the Hubrecht Imaging Center in Utrecht, staff test and optimise imaging experiments and train researchers in high-resolution imaging of living tissue, including cells inside live animals.

MRI and magnetic resonance spectroscopy at 7 tesla and above are at PRIME in Nijmegen, for living animals and for ex vivo samples. Micro-PET, micro-SPECT and their combinations with CT are also at PRIME. Micro-CT, micro-ultrasound and in vivo optical imaging are at PRIME as well. Intravital microscopy, which follows individual cells inside living animals, is at the Hubrecht Imaging Center in Utrecht.

## Imaging technologies offered

Most of the Node's methods image whole small animals at PRIME in Nijmegen. High-field MRI shows soft-tissue anatomy and physiology without sectioning, and magnetic resonance spectroscopy measures metabolites in the same scan. Because these methods do not harm the animal, researchers can repeat them to follow disease or treatment over time. PRIME also uses MRI at 7 tesla and above on ex vivo samples. Nuclear imaging with micro-PET and micro-SPECT measures where a radioactive tracer goes in the body and how its uptake changes over time. Combined micro-PET/CT and micro-SPECT-CT place that tracer signal within the animal's anatomy.

The supporting methods at PRIME add further readouts. Micro-CT gives three-dimensional anatomy, including bone and other mineralised structures. Micro-ultrasound shows organs, vessels and blood flow quickly and without ionising radiation, and in vivo optical imaging records fluorescent or bioluminescent reporters in the intact animal.

Intravital microscopy at the Hubrecht Imaging Center in Utrecht works at a different scale. It resolves individual cells and tissue structures in living animals, so researchers can watch cellular processes in their normal surroundings.

Instruments at PRIME include a small-bore 11.7T MRI for mice with a cryoprobe and a large-bore 7T MRI for mice and rats with in-bore focused ultrasound or cryo-ablation. Micro-PET/CT runs on a Siemens Inveon. The Hubrecht Imaging Center uses a Leica SP8 intravital microscope.

## Specialties and research focus

Research at PRIME centres on oncology, translational therapies, neuroscience and behaviour, and biopharmaceutical development. Studies there work with mice and rats, and PRIME also images human tissue ex vivo, which links animal work to human material. The centre's work on radiolabelled compounds and their testing in vitro and in vivo serves projects that develop new tracers and drugs. Photodynamic therapy is a further research area at PRIME, as are studies of behaviour and cognition in rodent models.

The Hubrecht Imaging Center in Utrecht serves research in developmental biology and cancer biology. In cancer research it supports intravital studies that follow tumour cells in living animals. Another focus is cell division, in particular mitosis and chromosomal instability, which researchers study through high-resolution imaging of living tissue.

Together the two facilities serve both translational questions, where imaging tracks disease and the response to treatment in whole animals, and cell biological questions about how cells behave inside living tissue.

## Services and user support

A project usually begins with methodological set-up, where staff help design the study protocol and write standard operating procedures. Researchers then receive training in the use of the infrastructure, supported by training workstations and a seminar room. During imaging, staff give technical assistance to run the instruments.

The Node prepares the materials and animals that imaging needs. Staff carry out probe preparation and animal preparation, and the Node provides animal facilities and housing for animals during a study. Visiting researchers also have wet lab space for their own bench work.

After acquisition, staff help with data processing and analysis, and server space holds the data. For projects that move towards clinical application, the Node offers regulatory affairs management, pharmacovigilance and contracting of clinical trial insurance.

PRIME at Radboudumc in Nijmegen is the Node's main preclinical imaging facility. It images small animals and human ex vivo tissue, and it brings MRI and spectroscopy, PET, SPECT, CT, ultrasound and optical imaging together for longitudinal and translational studies. The centre sits within Radboudumc's Central Animal Laboratory. A multidisciplinary team with expertise in radiology and nuclear medicine runs it.

The PRIME staff are experienced in combining several modalities on the same animal and in radiolabelling radiopharmaceuticals and testing them in vitro and in vivo. Their expertise also covers photodynamic therapy and studies of behaviour and cognition. The facility works with mice and rats and with human tissue ex vivo, and it serves research in oncology, neuroscience, translational therapies and biopharmaceutical development.

The Hubrecht Imaging Center, which runs the Intravital Microscopy Facility at the Hubrecht Institute in Utrecht, offers intravital microscopy for this Node. Its staff test and optimise imaging experiments, train researchers and support them during imaging, with a focus on high-resolution imaging of living tissue. The facility serves research in developmental biology, intravital cancer biology, and mitosis and chromosomal instability.
