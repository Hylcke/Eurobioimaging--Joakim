# Preclinical Imaging Centre (PRIME) - Molecular Imaging Dutch Node

## Overview

The PRIME Node is built around multimodality imaging of mice and rats. Its main facility, PRIME in Nijmegen, brings magnetic resonance, nuclear, X-ray, ultrasound and optical imaging together in one centre, so the same animals can be followed over time with several methods. The Node has two facilities at two institutions, Radboudumc in Nijmegen and the Hubrecht Institute in Utrecht, and it works entirely in biomedical imaging. The second facility, the Hubrecht Imaging Center in Utrecht, adds intravital microscopy, which looks at individual cells inside living animals.

PRIME is housed in Radboudumc's Central Animal Laboratory. A multidisciplinary team with roots in radiology and nuclear medicine runs it. The staff have experience in radiolabelling radiopharmaceuticals and testing them in vitro and in vivo. This links tracer chemistry directly to studies with positron emission tomography (PET) and single-photon emission computed tomography (SPECT). They also bring expertise in photodynamic therapy and in behaviour and cognition studies, and they image human ex vivo tissue as well as small animals. At the Hubrecht Imaging Center, staff test and optimise imaging approaches and support experiments that need high-resolution imaging of living tissue.

Groups running longitudinal studies of tumours, new therapies or brain function in mice and rats will find most support at PRIME. Translational and biopharmaceutical projects fit there well, because tracer expertise and several imaging modalities sit under one roof. Developmental and cancer biologists who need to watch cells behave inside a living animal should turn to the Hubrecht Imaging Center. Projects that need both scales can use PRIME for whole-body imaging and the Hubrecht Imaging Center for the cellular view.

## Imaging technologies offered

Most of the Node's methods are at PRIME, and they image whole small animals. High-field magnetic resonance imaging and spectroscopy (MRI/MRS) at 7 tesla and above shows soft-tissue anatomy and physiology without sectioning. It can also measure metabolites, and scans can be repeated in the same animal. PRIME runs a large-bore 7T MRI for mice and rats with in-bore focused ultrasound or cryo-ablation, so a treatment can be delivered inside the scanner while the animal is imaged. A small-bore 11.7T MRI with a cryoprobe serves mouse studies and is also used for ex vivo MRI of specimens.

For tracer studies, PRIME offers micro-PET and micro-SPECT, and each is also available combined with micro-CT. PET gives sensitive measurements of where a tracer goes and how quickly. SPECT works with gamma-emitting isotopes, and the added CT places either signal within the animal's anatomy. Micro-PET/CT runs on a Siemens Inveon system. Micro-CT on its own gives 3D anatomy, including mineralised structures such as bone.

Two supporting methods at PRIME complete the picture. Micro-ultrasound shows structure and blood flow without ionising radiation, and in vivo optical imaging reads out fluorescent or bioluminescent reporters across the whole animal. For cellular detail, the Hubrecht Imaging Center in Utrecht offers intravital microscopy on a Leica SP8 intravital system, which follows cells and tissue structures in accessible regions of a living animal.

## Specialties and research focus

Research at PRIME centres on oncology, translational therapies, neuroscience and behaviour, and biopharmaceutical development. Its studies use mice and rats, and the setup of each instrument determines which species it takes. The centre is designed for longitudinal, multimodal and translational studies, which follow disease or the response to treatment in the same animals over time. Because PRIME also images human ex vivo tissue, findings in animal models can be set beside human material. Radiopharmaceutical development and photodynamic therapy are further areas of work, and behaviour and cognition studies tie imaging to brain function.

The Hubrecht Imaging Center in Utrecht serves a different scientific community. Its work spans developmental biology and cancer biology, where intravital imaging shows tumour cells in their living surroundings. A further focus there is mitosis and chromosomal instability, which concerns how cells divide and how errors arise when chromosomes are shared between daughter cells.

## Services and user support

A project starts with methodological setup, where staff help design the study protocol and the standard operating procedures. Researchers can then be trained to use the infrastructure, with training workstations and a seminar room set aside for this. Alternatively, they can work with staff who give technical assistance in running the instruments.

Preparation is part of the service. Staff prepare probes and animals, and the Node provides animal facilities, housing facilities and wet lab space for the experimental work around each imaging session. Once images are acquired, staff help with data processing and analysis, and server space is available for the data. For studies moving towards clinical use, the Node also offers regulatory affairs management, pharmacovigilance and the contracting of clinical trial insurance.

PRIME in Nijmegen is the larger of the two facilities and offers almost all of the Node's technologies. It is a centralised facility within Radboudumc, housed in the Central Animal Laboratory, and its team draws on both radiology and nuclear medicine. The centre covers magnetic resonance, nuclear, X-ray, ultrasound and optical imaging. Because these modalities share one centre, a study can combine anatomical, functional and molecular readouts in the same animals without moving them between institutes. PRIME suits projects that track a disease or a therapy through time, and its work with human ex vivo tissue supports studies that bridge animal models and patients.

The Hubrecht Imaging Center in Utrecht is part of the Hubrecht Institute and is also known as the Intravital Microscopy Facility. Through the Node it offers intravital microscopy. Its staff test and optimise imaging approaches with users and support experiments that image living tissue at high resolution. It suits questions about how individual cells behave in their natural setting, during development or in a growing tumour.

The two sites work at different scales. PRIME looks at whole animals and organs, while the Hubrecht Imaging Center resolves single cells inside them. A cancer project could follow tumour growth and tracer uptake at PRIME and then study cell behaviour within the tumour at the Hubrecht Imaging Center.
