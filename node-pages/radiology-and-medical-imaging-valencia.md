# Radiology and Medical Imaging Valencia

## Overview

Radiology and Medical Imaging Valencia is a biomedical imaging Node built around quantitative imaging biomarkers. These are reproducible measurements drawn from MRI and PET images with radiomics and artificial intelligence. Its centre of gravity is the La Fe hospital and research institute in Valencia, where clinical and preclinical scanners sit next to the teams that analyse the images. The Node brings together six facilities at six institutions in Valencia, Madrid and Sant Joan d’Alacant. It covers preclinical imaging of animals and tissue as well as clinical imaging of human participants, and it puts unusual weight on image data and analysis.

The staff are experienced in turning images into measurements that hold up across studies. The Biomedical Imaging Research Group in Valencia works on imaging biomarkers, radiomics and deep learning for medical images. It also runs a research data warehouse that links radiology, pathology and laboratory data and is updated every day. The warehouse maps clinical terms to standard vocabularies such as ICD-10 and SNOMED and stores data in the OMOP Common Data Model, which the group has extended for imaging with the OHDSI community. BIMCV in Valencia curates large medical imaging datasets for AI research, including PADCHEST and BIMCV-COVID19. At the preclinical end, the Biomedical NMR Sebastián Cerdán Facility in Madrid brings long experience with magnetic resonance and metabolomic studies in small animals.

Clinical researchers running imaging studies or trials in oncology and neurology will find most support at PREBI and Imaging La Fe, both in Valencia. Groups assembling large retrospective cohorts or developing AI models should start with PREBI, which supports population imaging studies. Preclinical magnetic resonance projects can go to PREBI or to the Biomedical NMR Sebastián Cerdán Facility in Madrid.

## Imaging technologies offered

Magnetic resonance runs through most of the Node. For human studies, Imaging La Fe and PREBI both offer MRI and MR spectroscopy below 7 T. These show soft-tissue anatomy and physiology and can measure metabolites in a chosen volume of tissue, all without ionising radiation. The same two facilities offer PET-MRI, which records the distribution of a radioactive tracer and the MR images in a single session. Imaging La Fe runs a Philips Achieva 3.0T TX and a GE SIGNA 3T PET/MRI, while PREBI uses a GE SIGNA for simultaneous PET and 3 T MR in clinical research and trials.

Preclinical work follows the same pattern on a smaller scale. Micro-MRI and MRS below 7 T are offered by PREBI and by the Biomedical NMR Sebastián Cerdán Facility, whose systems include a Bruker BioSpec 70/16. PREBI also images excised organs and tissues ex vivo by micro-MRI. Its Bruker Albira micro-PET/CT pairs tracer imaging with X-ray CT, so tracer uptake in small animals can be read against the surrounding anatomy. Alongside acquisition, PREBI provides advanced support for population imaging studies. This covers cohort set-up, secure data preparation and storage, processing and tailored software, and the platform also offers medical image analysis.

## Specialties and research focus

Oncology and neurology are the shared fields across the Node. PREBI and Imaging La Fe apply clinical and preclinical imaging to biomarker research in both areas, and Imaging La Fe focuses on the discovery and validation of new biomarkers in translational studies that move between patients and animal models. The Biomedical Imaging Research Group connects this work to clinical trials, radiomics and imaging biomarkers in human and animal studies.

The preclinical sites add their own emphases. The Biomedical NMR Sebastián Cerdán Facility supports research in oncology, neurology, immunology and drug development, including metabolomic studies in small animals. The Magnetic Resonance and Molecular Imaging Facility in Sant Joan d’Alacant works with rodents and serves neuroscience, biomarker development and translational imaging.

BIMCV brings a population view. Its datasets come from projects on prostate cancer, brain tumours such as glioma, multiple sclerosis, wider neuroimaging and chest imaging during COVID-19. Together these give researchers material for training and testing AI models on real clinical images from the Valencia Region.

## Services and user support

A project usually begins with study design and management. Staff help shape the imaging protocol and plan how the study will run, and they stay involved as the project develops. For clinical research, the Node can coordinate clinical trials or take part in them, which suits studies that need imaging across several time points or patient groups.

Once images are acquired, staff assist with image processing, data analysis and the interpretation of results. This is where the Node's background in radiomics and imaging biomarkers is most useful, because the aim is measurements that can be compared across subjects and studies. The Node provides data storage and computational resources for this work. It also operates as a federated node for distributed analysis, which helps multicentre projects whose data sit at different sites. Finally, the Node offers 3D printing of models, so a project can produce physical models alongside its images and measurements.

### Services offered

- User-oriented project support with study design and management
- Assistance in image processing, data analyses, and interpretation
- Federated node for distributed analysis
- Data storage
- Computational resources
- Coordination and participation in clinical trials
- 3D printing models

Four of the six facilities are in Valencia and are closely linked through the La Fe hospital and its research institute. Imaging La Fe is the hospital-based infrastructure within the ReDIB network, combining clinical imaging with the PREBI platform and the Biomedical Imaging Research Group. PREBI offers the most methods in the Node and follows a study from acquisition through processing to quantified biomarkers. It also images large animals and tissue on its 3 T MRI system, alongside separate preclinical equipment. The Biomedical Imaging Research Group is a service unit that supports imaging research across preclinical and human studies and runs the research data warehouse. BIMCV, the Medical Imaging Databank of the Valencia Region, is a data resource rather than an acquisition site and hosts curated imaging datasets for research and AI.

Two facilities outside Valencia extend the preclinical side. The Biomedical NMR Sebastián Cerdán Facility in Madrid belongs to the Instituto de Investigaciones Biomedicas Sols-Morreale (CSIC-UAM) and gives scientific advice and technical support for small-animal magnetic resonance and spectroscopy. The Magnetic Resonance and Molecular Imaging Facility at the Instituto de Neurociencias CSIC-UMH supports in vivo and ex vivo studies in rodents, with a focus on translational neuroscience. Projects can therefore combine animal work at these sites with clinical imaging and data analysis in Valencia.
