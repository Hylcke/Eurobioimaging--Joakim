# Radiology and Medical Imaging Valencia

## Overview

Radiology and Medical Imaging Valencia is a biomedical imaging Node built around magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and PET-MRI in patients and small animals. Its second focus is the quantitative analysis of medical images, in particular the extraction of reproducible imaging biomarkers with radiomics and artificial intelligence. The Node has six facilities at six institutions in Valencia, Madrid and Sant Joan d'Alacant, and it covers both preclinical and clinical imaging.

Much of the Node's expertise sits with the Biomedical Imaging Research Group (GIBI230) at IIS La Fe in Valencia. Its staff work on imaging biomarkers, radiomics and deep learning for medical imaging. The group runs a research data warehouse that is updated daily and links radiology, pathology and laboratory data. Terms are mapped to standard vocabularies such as SNOMED and ICD-10, and the data sit in the OMOP Common Data Model, which the group has extended for imaging with the OHDSI community. Staff can therefore assemble retrospective datasets of thousands of records within days. BIMCV in Valencia curates large imaging datasets for machine learning, including PADCHEST and BIMCV-COVID19. Staff at the Biomedical NMR Sebastián Cerdán Facility in Madrid are experienced in magnetic resonance spectroscopy and metabolomic studies, while the Magnetic Resonance and Molecular Imaging Facility in Sant Joan d'Alacant works with rodents in translational neuroscience.

Clinical MRI, magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS) and PET-MRI are at Imaging La Fe and PREBI, both in Valencia. Preclinical MRI and MRS are at the Biomedical NMR Sebastián Cerdán Facility in Madrid and PREBI in Valencia. PREBI also offers small-animal micro-PET/CT, ex vivo MRI of tissues and support for population imaging studies.

## Imaging technologies offered

Magnetic resonance is the core of the Node, in both patients and animals. Human MRI and MRS show soft-tissue anatomy, tissue physiology and metabolite levels within each voxel, without ionising radiation. Imaging La Fe and PREBI in Valencia also offer PET-MRI, which places the distribution of a radioactive tracer in its anatomical and physiological context. Both run a GE SIGNA scanner that acquires PET and 3 T MRI at the same time, and Imaging La Fe adds a Philips Achieva 3.0T TX for MRI.

Preclinical MRI and MRS follow anatomy, physiology and metabolism in living animals, and the same animal can be scanned several times during a study. The Biomedical NMR Sebastián Cerdán Facility in Madrid uses an MR Solutions PET/MRI system that works at 3 T or 7 T, and PREBI offers the same methods in Valencia. PREBI also images excised organs and tissues by ex vivo MRI and MRS. For tracer studies in small animals, PREBI runs micro-PET/CT on a Bruker Albira, which combines tracer distribution with X-ray anatomy and attenuation correction.

Beyond image acquisition, PREBI supports population imaging studies, from setting up a cohort to the secure preparation, storage and processing of its data with tailored software. It also offers quantitative analysis of medical images.

## Specialties and research focus

The research these facilities serve is mainly in oncology and neurology. BIMCV in Valencia supports projects on prostate cancer and brain tumours such as glioma, on chest imaging including COVID-19, and on multiple sclerosis and neuroimaging. Imaging La Fe works on human and preclinical translational imaging in radiology, oncology and neurology, with a focus on discovering and validating biomarkers. PREBI serves oncology, neurology and wider biomarker research in both clinical and preclinical studies.

The Biomedical NMR Sebastián Cerdán Facility in Madrid supports oncology, neurology, immunology and drug development, and it carries out metabolomic studies alongside preclinical imaging. The Magnetic Resonance and Molecular Imaging Facility in Sant Joan d'Alacant serves neuroscience and biomarker development in rodent models, together with translational imaging. In Valencia, the Biomedical Imaging Research Group turns images into quantitative biomarkers using radiomics, artificial intelligence and population imaging. Its main aim is to strengthen observational studies, and it also works on clinical trials and preclinical animal imaging.

## Services and user support

A project with the Node starts with user-oriented project support, in which staff help design the study and then manage it as it runs. For clinical research, the Node coordinates and takes part in clinical trials, so imaging can be built into a trial from the start.

Once images are acquired, staff assist with image processing, data analysis and the interpretation of results. Researchers can draw on the Node's computational resources for this work, and data can be stored at the Node. The Node also operates as a federated node for distributed analysis, which allows analyses to run across data held in different places rather than in a single store. This connects with the Node's work on large retrospective and population datasets, where data often come from several sources. For projects that need a physical object, such as a model of an organ or structure, the Node also produces 3D printing models.

- Assistance in image processing, data analyses, and interpretation
- Federated node for distributed analysis
- Data storage

- Coordination and participation in clinical trials
- 3D printing models

Clinical imaging is based at the hospital and research institute of La Fe in Valencia. Imaging La Fe is a hospital-based infrastructure within the ReDIB network that brings together clinical imaging, the PREBI platform and the GIBI230 research group. Its staff work on clinical and preclinical imaging, big-data mining and radiomics. PREBI offers imaging from acquisition through to the quantification of imaging biomarkers, in both clinical and preclinical studies. It provides every method the Node offers, and it supports large-animal and tissue imaging on a 3 T MRI system.

Two further facilities focus on preclinical work. The Biomedical NMR Sebastián Cerdán Facility in Madrid gives scientific advice and technical support for small-animal MRI and MRS, and its staff are experienced in in vivo, in vitro and ex vivo NMR. The Magnetic Resonance and Molecular Imaging Facility in Sant Joan d'Alacant supports in vivo and ex vivo studies in rodents, with expertise in imaging biomarkers and translational neuroscience.

Two Valencia groups centre on data and analysis. The Biomedical Imaging Research Group (GIBI230) at IIS La Fe is a combined clinical and technical unit that develops imaging biomarkers, radiomics and deep learning methods, and it supports projects from acquisition to quantitative analysis. BIMCV, the Medical Imaging Databank of the Valencia Region, hosts large curated imaging datasets for research and artificial intelligence.
