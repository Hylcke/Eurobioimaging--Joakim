# SiMBION Node

## Overview

The SiMBION Node is Slovenia's national bioimaging consortium, and its main strength is the microscopy of cells and tissues by confocal and electron microscopy. Five of its facilities offer electron microscopy and five offer confocal microscopy, so a project can move from fluorescence to ultrastructure within one network. The Node joins 15 facilities at nine institutions in Ljubljana and Maribor. These facilities grew out of separate institutional initiatives and now operate as a single distributed Node. It covers both biological and biomedical imaging, and it also offers chemical imaging of tissue sections and magnetic resonance microscopy of excised samples.

Much of the staff expertise lies in preparing biological material for microscopy. Staff at the Centre for Electron Microscopy in Ljubljana are experienced in chemical fixation and preparation for transmission electron microscopy, and they also work with cryo-immobilised samples. BMCM in Ljubljana specialises in biomedical microscopy and sample preparation. The National Institute of Biology in Ljubljana brings experience with plant and other biological samples, and the Infrastructural Center Microscopy of Biological Samples in Ljubljana handles material from viruses and microorganisms to plants and animals. Beyond microscopy, the JSI Microanalytical Centre in Ljubljana develops accelerated-ion-beam techniques, and the Jozef Stefan Institute in Ljubljana specialises in magnetic resonance microscopy. Two further centres add expertise in research data management and bioinformatics.

Cell biologists who want to combine fluorescence and ultrastructure in one project will find most support at the Centre for Electron Microscopy, which offers confocal microscopy and TEM in one place. Plant scientists can turn to the National Institute of Biology, which pairs confocal microscopy and TEM with expertise in plant biology. Researchers who need maps of elements or molecules in tissue sections should look to the JSI Microanalytical Centre.

## Imaging technologies offered

Confocal and electron microscopy form the core of the Node. Laser scanning confocal microscopy gives optical sections and multichannel 3D localisation of structures in cells and tissues, and five facilities offer it. The Centre for Electron Microscopy runs a Zeiss LSM 900 Airyscan for this work. BMCM adds total internal reflection fluorescence (TIRF) microscopy, which images only the region next to the coverslip with high contrast and so suits membrane events and single molecules.

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) of chemically fixed samples shows cellular ultrastructure in detail, and the Centre for Electron Microscopy and the National Institute of Biology both offer it. The Centre for Electron Microscopy also offers TEM of cryo-immobilised samples. Negative-stain TEM for checking particle morphology is offered by BMCM, the Centre for Electron Microscopy and the National Institute of Biology. The National Institute of Biology also offers immunolabelling on immobilised particles. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) of surface morphology is offered by BMCM, UCEM and the Infrastructural Center Microscopy of Biological Samples.

Chemical imaging forms a smaller group. The JSI Microanalytical Centre is built around a 2 MV Tandetron accelerator and maps elements with micro-particle induced X-ray emission (µ-PIXE) and surface molecules with MeV secondary ion mass spectrometry (MeV-SIMS). Mass spectrometry-based imaging of lipids, metabolites and peptides is offered by the JSI Microanalytical Centre and the National Institute of Chemistry. The Jozef Stefan Institute performs MRI and MR spectroscopy of excised specimens below and above 7 T, including on a 9.4 T vertical system.

## Specialties and research focus

The research the Node serves spans biomedicine, plant science and several applied fields. On the biomedical side, BMCM supports cell and molecular biology, diagnostics and studies of targeted drug delivery into cancer cells and pathogen-infected cells. The Center for Microscopy and Image Analysis in Ljubljana serves veterinary medicine and other biomedical sciences. The Center for Clinical Physiology in Ljubljana contributes to clinical physiology, neuroscience, human imaging and vascular and metabolic research.

Plant and environmental science form a second focus. The National Institute of Biology works in plant biology, biotechnology, systems biology and environmental sciences. The Chair of Botany and Plant Physiology in Ljubljana studies plant physiology and ecology, with a particular interest in ionomics and element mapping. The JSI Microanalytical Centre maps elements in biomedical tissue and in plant material such as wheat grain, and it also works on materials and archaeometry. The National Institute of Chemistry analyses environmental, biomedical, industrial and pharmaceutical samples. The Jozef Stefan Institute applies MR microscopy to the 3D anatomy of tooth pulp, tumour imaging, water distribution in wood and foods such as dry-cured ham and bread dough.

## Services and user support

Work with the Node usually begins with methodological setup, where staff help design the study protocol and standard operating procedures. Researchers can then be trained to use the instruments, with training workstations and a seminar room to support this. Alternatively, they can work with technical assistance from staff who run the instrument for them.

On the preparation side, staff can prepare probes and animals. The Node also provides animal facilities, housing facilities, wet lab space and cell culture facilities at safety level 1. Biological material can be stored and processed through the Node's biobanking service. Projects with a clinical side receive further support, which covers clinical trial insurance contracting, pharmacovigilance and regulatory affairs management. Once images are acquired, staff help with data processing and analysis, and the Node provides server space for project data.

Most of the Node sits in Ljubljana, where 13 of its 15 facilities are based. The University of Ljubljana Faculty of Medicine hosts the largest cluster. BMCM is the faculty's infrastructural microscopy centre and is chaired by the Institute of Cell Biology. It connects the units that image biological material, so expertise and equipment are shared between them. The same faculty hosts the Carl Zeiss Reference Center for Confocal Microscopy, which supports cellular and molecular biology research. The Center for Clinical Physiology adds expertise in clinical physiology and human imaging. The Infrastructure Center for Data Management handles research data following FAIR principles, and the ELIXIR-SI Center contributes bioinformatics.

At the Biotechnical Faculty, the Infrastructural Center Microscopy of Biological Samples supports the preparation and observation of biological samples. The Chair of Botany and Plant Physiology adds sample preparation and data analysis for plant element mapping, which fits alongside the ion-beam work of the JSI Microanalytical Centre. The Jozef Stefan Institute contributes two facilities, the Microanalytical Centre and the Laboratory for Magnetic Resonance Microscopy. The National Institute of Chemistry complements them with mass spectrometry-based imaging. The Center for Microscopy and Image Analysis at the Veterinary Faculty brings veterinary expertise and confocal microscopy. In Maribor, UCEM offers scanning electron microscopy, and the University Medical Centre Maribor imaging services contribute clinical imaging capability to the network.
