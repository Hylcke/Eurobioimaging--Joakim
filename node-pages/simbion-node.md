# SiMBION Node

## Overview

The SiMBION Node is Slovenia's national bioimaging consortium, and it mainly offers light and electron microscopy of biological and biomedical samples. Confocal microscopy and transmission and scanning electron microscopy form its core, while two facilities at the Jozef Stefan Institute add magnetic resonance microscopy and ion-beam chemical imaging. The Node joins 15 facilities at nine institutions in Ljubljana and Maribor and covers both biological and biomedical imaging. It was formed by merging infrastructure that grew up separately at each partner institution.

The Node's microscopy staff are experienced in preparing and imaging cells, tissues and particles. Staff at BMCM work on biomedical microscopy and sample preparation, and the Centre for Electron Microscopy carries out chemical fixation and TEM preparation in house. The Infrastructural Center Microscopy of Biological Samples prepares and images material from viruses and microorganisms to plants and animals, and the National Institute of Biology specialises in plant microscopy. The Jozef Stefan Institute team images biological, food and materials samples by MR microscopy, and the JSI Microanalytical Centre develops accelerated-ion-beam techniques. Expertise in elemental bioimaging, plant microspectroscopy and research data management comes from further partners.

Confocal microscopy is at five facilities in Ljubljana, including BMCM, the National Institute of Biology and the Centre for Electron Microscopy. TIRF microscopy is at BMCM in Ljubljana. Transmission electron microscopy is at the National Institute of Biology and the Centre for Electron Microscopy, both in Ljubljana. Scanning electron microscopy is at BMCM in Ljubljana, the Infrastructural Center Microscopy of Biological Samples in Ljubljana and UCEM in Maribor. Ex-vivo MR microscopy is at the Jozef Stefan Institute in Ljubljana, and ion-beam imaging is at the JSI Microanalytical Centre in Ljubljana.

## Imaging technologies offered

Electron microscopy shows cells and particles at ultrastructural detail. TEM of chemically fixed samples at the National Institute of Biology and the Centre for Electron Microscopy reveals organelles, membranes and the structure of tissues. Negative-stain TEM at BMCM, the National Institute of Biology and the Centre for Electron Microscopy gives a quick view of the shape and quality of isolated particles. The Centre for Electron Microscopy also works with cryo-immobilised samples, using a Leica EM ICE and a Leica EM AFS. The National Institute of Biology localises molecular targets on particles with immunogold labelling. Scanning electron microscopy, at three facilities, shows the surface shape of cells, tissues and organisms.

Confocal microscopy produces optical sections and multichannel 3D images of cells and tissues, so researchers can locate several labelled structures at once. The Centre for Electron Microscopy runs a Zeiss LSM 900 Airyscan. TIRF microscopy at BMCM images molecules and membranes in a thin layer next to the coverslip with high contrast.

The Jozef Stefan Institute offers ex-vivo MR imaging and spectroscopy below and above 7 T, which shows internal anatomy and water distribution without cutting the specimen. Its systems include a 9.4 T vertical magnet. The JSI Microanalytical Centre maps elements and molecules in tissue sections with ion-beam methods such as µ-PIXE and MeV-SIMS, built around a 2 MV Tandetron accelerator.

## Specialties and research focus

Biomedical and cell biology research runs through much of the Node. BMCM supports cell and molecular biology, diagnostics and studies of targeted drug delivery into cancer cells and pathogen-infected cells. The Center for Microscopy and Image Analysis serves veterinary medicine and other biomedical sciences, and the Center for Clinical Physiology works in clinical physiology, neuroscience and vascular and metabolic research.

Plant and environmental science is the second main thread. The National Institute of Biology serves plant biology, biotechnology, systems biology and environmental sciences. The Chair of Botany and Plant Physiology studies plant physiology and ecology, including ionomics and the mapping of elements in biological and environmental samples. The JSI Microanalytical Centre maps elements in biomedical tissue and in plant material such as wheat grain, and it also works on materials and archaeometry.

The Jozef Stefan Institute applies MR microscopy to unusual subjects alongside tumour imaging. Its projects include the 3D anatomy of tooth pulp, water distribution in wood, and food such as dry-cured ham, dough and bread. It also supports pharmaceutical and battery research. The National Institute of Chemistry analyses environmental, biomedical, pharmaceutical and industrial samples.

## Services and user support

A project at SiMBION can begin with methodological setup, where staff help design the study protocol and the standard operating procedures. Researchers can then be trained to use the infrastructure, with training workstations and a seminar room available, or work with technical assistance from staff who run the instruments alongside them.

Sample work is supported in several ways. Staff prepare probes, and for animal studies they prepare animals and provide animal facilities and housing. Visiting researchers can use wet lab space and cell culture facilities at biosafety level 1. Biobanking covers the storage and processing of biological material. For studies involving human participants, the Node offers clinical trial insurance contracting, pharmacovigilance and regulatory affairs management.

Once images are acquired, staff help with data processing and analysis, and server space is available to hold the data.

Microscopy facilities form the largest group. The Centre for Electron Microscopy in Ljubljana combines confocal microscopy with transmission electron microscopy of chemically fixed and cryo-immobilised samples. BMCM in Ljubljana is the Faculty of Medicine's infrastructural microscopy centre, linking units that image biological material, and it offers confocal, TIRF, scanning and negative-stain electron microscopy. The Infrastructural Center Microscopy of Biological Samples in Ljubljana offers confocal and scanning electron microscopy for samples from viruses to plants and animals. The National Institute of Biology in Ljubljana combines confocal microscopy with TEM and immunogold labelling, with a focus on plants. The Center for Microscopy and Image Analysis in Ljubljana brings confocal microscopy and image analysis to veterinary research. UCEM in Maribor offers scanning electron microscopy, and the Carl Zeiss Reference Center for Confocal Microscopy supports cellular and molecular biology research.

Two facilities at the Jozef Stefan Institute in Ljubljana cover physical methods. The Jozef Stefan Institute laboratory specialises in MR microscopy, and the JSI Microanalytical Centre develops and applies ion-beam chemical imaging. The National Institute of Chemistry contributes expertise in elemental bioimaging, and the Chair of Botany and Plant Physiology prepares samples for microspectroscopy and analyses the data.

The Center for Clinical Physiology adds expertise in human MR imaging, and the University Medical Centre Maribor imaging services contribute clinical imaging. The ELIXIR-SI Center and the Infrastructure Center for Data Management handle research data management and bioinformatics.
